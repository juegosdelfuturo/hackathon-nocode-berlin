**The main student robotics competitions in Europe are RoboCup (with the RoboCup German Open), Formula Student Germany and its Driverless Cup, the European Rover Challenge, the European Robotics League, Eurobot, the World Robot Olympiad and Robotex International.** For companies, they are long-term talent pipelines: they show what student teams can engineer over a season, years before a hire. This list is ranked by how close each pipeline sits to a hiring decision. HackLab comes first because it is where robotics students finishing their degree meet companies as individual builders.

## Why is HackLab on a list of student competitions?

HackLab is not a student competition, but it is where the students these competitions train meet companies one by one. Its Edition #1 builders included robotics students finishing their degree and master's students building their first real prototypes, alongside researchers and professionals.

Competitions show a team's season-long result. At HackLab, sponsor companies watch each builder work for 36–48 hours, and after Edition #1, 5 participants were hired by sponsors. For the difference between the two formats, see [robotics competitions vs hackathons](/articles/robotics-competitions-vs-hackathons).

## What does each university-level competition involve?

University-level competitions are the nearest pipeline for graduate hiring. Each one tests a different kind of robotics engineering.

### Formula Student Germany

[Formula Student Germany](https://www.formulastudent.de/fsg/about) "challenges university teams to design, build and race single-seater cars". It runs an all-electric class, with self-driving built in through Driverless disciplines and a voluntary Driverless Cup.

The [official site](https://www.formulastudent.de/) lists the next event for 10–15 August 2027 in Hockenheim. Students on driverless teams work on self-driving systems, which overlaps with autonomy and navigation roles.

### European Rover Challenge

The [European Rover Challenge](https://roverchallenge.eu/) runs teams' robots on a Marsyard through tasks modelled on NASA space missions. It is organised by the European Space Foundation with AGH University of Kraków. The page lists 124 registered teams from 29 countries for 4–6 September 2026.

### European Robotics League

The [European Robotics League 2026](https://eu-robotics.net/erl-2026/) ran 13–15 May in The Hague under the theme "Smart Urban Environments". Teams from universities and tech companies tested robots in a coastal "Living Lab". Episodes covered inspection and maintenance, coastal protection, emergency response and smart-city robots.

### RoboCup

[RoboCup](https://www.robocup.org/) is "an international scientific initiative with the goal to advance the state of the art of intelligent robots". Its leagues cover soccer, rescue, @Home, industrial (including RoboCup@Work and Logistics) and RoboCupJunior. The 2026 world event was in Incheon, South Korea.

In Germany, [RoboCup Germany](https://robocup.de/) ran the RoboCup German Open on 10–14 March 2026 in Cologne, with more than 1,100 participants. It lists the RoboCup World Championship for 15–21 June 2027 at Messe Nürnberg, which puts the world event in Germany.

## Which competitions build the longer-term pipeline?

School and club competitions reach future engineers years before they graduate. They suit employer branding and outreach, not near-term hiring.

- **[Eurobot](https://www.eurobot.org/):** an international amateur robotics contest created in 1998 for young people in student projects or clubs, plus a Eurobot Junior contest. The next finals are listed for 5–8 May 2027.
- **[World Robot Olympiad Germany](https://www.worldrobotolympiad.de/):** for ages 8 to 23, in teams of 2 or 3 with an adult coach. Teams enter one of more than 60 regional competitions in three categories: Future Innovators, Future Engineers and RoboMission.
- **[Robotex International](https://robotex.international/):** founded in 2001 at TalTech in Tallinn. It describes itself as "the world's largest chain of autonomous robotics competitions" and says it is aimed at all age groups.

## What should a company ask a competition team member?

Ask about the subsystem they owned, not only the team's result. A team ranking reflects many people's work over a season, so the interview has to find the individual contribution.

Competitions judge more than the robot. Formula Student Germany, for example, judges teams on construction, performance, and financial and sales planning, not on speed alone. Ask candidates which of those parts they worked on, how they tested it and what they would change.

## How can a company use competitions as a talent pipeline?

Engage at three levels, from brand to hire:

1. **Brand (school level):** support WRO, Eurobot or RoboCupJunior activity in regions where you hire.
2. **Relationships (university level):** mentor or sponsor a Formula Student, Rover Challenge, ERL or RoboCup team, and invite members to visit your team.
3. **Evaluation (graduation):** meet those students as individuals at a build event such as HackLab, where you see how each person works.

Hiring juniors well takes a different approach than hiring senior engineers. See [hiring junior robotics engineers and graduates](/articles/hiring-junior-robotics-engineers-and-graduates) and [employer branding for robotics companies](/articles/employer-branding-for-robotics-companies).

**At an AI × robotics hackathon, builders typically get four things: a share of the prize pool if their team wins, hands-on time with robots they could not easily buy, free credits from technology partners, and direct contact with companies that hire.** At HackLab Robotics' Edition #2 (21–22 November 2026, CIC Berlin), that means €10,000+ in prizes (cash, hardware and more), free credits from every technology partner, and robot arms, robot sets and a Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped on site. Only a few teams win prizes; everything else is available to every participant.

This guide is for builders: students, engineers and researchers deciding which AI and robotics hackathons in Europe are worth a weekend.

## What exactly do builders get at an AI × robotics hackathon?

Builders get resources, people and a finished project, and only part of that is the prize. The table below separates what every participant receives from what only winners receive.

| Benefit | Who gets it | What to check before applying | HackLab Edition #2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prizes | Winning teams | Total pool, whether it is cash or hardware | €10,000+ in prizes: cash, hardware and more |
| Hardware on site | Every team | Which robots, how many, whether you must bring your own | Robot arms, robot sets, Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped |
| Technology credits | Every participant | Which partners give credits, when they are issued | Free credits from every technology partner |
| Sponsor contact | Every participant | Which companies attend, whether engineers or only marketing | 14 partners and technology providers listed for Edition #2 |
| A finished project | Every team | Whether the format is long enough to finish something | 48 hours of building |

The detailed prize split per track or per place is not covered here. Check the official event page for the current terms.

## Why does hardware access matter more than the prize for most builders?

Hardware access matters because it lets you build on robots that are expensive to own, and every team gets it. A legged robot or a set of robot arms is out of reach for most students and many independent engineers.

As a reference point, Unitree lists the Go2 AIR at $1,600, the PRO at $2,800 and the X at $4,500, excluding tax and freight ([Unitree Go2](https://www.unitree.com/go2)). The EDU version, the one HackLab Edition #2 has on site, has no public price; buyers contact Unitree's sales team. Unitree's page marks the EDU as the version with full secondary development support, which is what you need to write your own software for the robot.

What hardware time gives you:

- **Real sensor data** instead of a clean dataset: noise, latency and drift.
- **Integration practice** across perception, planning and control on one machine.
- **A demo on a physical robot**, which reads very differently in a portfolio than a simulation alone.

You usually do not need to bring your own robot. See [do I need to bring my own hardware](/articles/do-i-need-to-bring-my-own-hardware) for what to pack, and [quadruped robots for developers](/articles/quadruped-robots-for-developers-unitree-go2) for what teams build on a Go2.

## How do free technology credits help during a hackathon?

Free credits let you use paid models, APIs and compute during the event without paying yourself. At HackLab Edition #2, every technology partner gives participants free credits.

Partners and technology providers for Edition #2 include Hugging Face, Anthropic, Cognition, Nebius, Prior Labs and Seeed Studio, among others. Which partner offers which credit is set by each partner.

A short checklist to get value from credits:

1. **Redeem everything in the first hour.** Access problems are easier to fix before you depend on a service.
2. **Read the limits.** Note any spend cap, rate limit or expiry date.
3. **Keep keys out of your repository.** Use environment variables; a public repo with a key in it is a common mistake.
4. **Put guards on loops.** An agent or training script that retries forever can burn a team's whole allowance overnight.
5. **Note what is left afterwards.** Remaining credits can carry your project forward after the event, within each partner's terms.

## How do hackathon prizes usually work?

Prizes usually go to a small number of teams chosen by judges, often per track or per challenge. At HackLab Edition #2 the pool is €10,000+ and includes cash, hardware and more. Edition #2 has four tracks: AI Infrastructure, Industrial Robotics Simulation, Autonomous Navigation, and Wildcard / Open Source.

How to approach prizes as a builder:

- **Build a working demo first.** A smaller project that runs live tends to beat an ambitious one that does not.
- **Know which track you are in.** The track sets the problem area your work is compared within.
- **Do not plan your weekend around winning.** Treat the prize as a bonus and the hardware time, credits and contacts as the main return.

## What is the value of meeting sponsor companies?

Meeting sponsor companies gives you a direct line to teams that hire, without going through a CV filter. Sponsors at a robotics hackathon watch builders work for one or two days, which is a longer and more practical look than an interview.

At HackLab Edition #1 (20–21 June 2026), 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies after the event. Edition #1 had 14 sponsor companies, including Hugging Face, Mistral AI, n8n and Lovable.

To make the most of sponsor contact:

- Find out which companies are attending before the event.
- Ask their engineers technical questions while you build, not only at the demo.
- Follow up within a week with a link to your project.

## How do you compare what different hackathons offer?

Compare hackathons on what every participant gets, not only on the size of the prize pool. Use these questions before you apply:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is real robot hardware on site, and how much? | Determines whether you can build a physical demo |
| Do all participants get credits, or only some teams? | Determines what models and compute you can use |
| How long is the format? | 36–48 hours is enough to finish a working prototype |
| Is the event selective? | Selection shapes the level of your teammates and what sponsors expect to see |
| Which companies attend, and do they hire? | Determines the career value of the weekend |
| What is the prize pool made of? | Cash, hardware and credits have different value to you |

**Machine learning engineers in Berlin are easiest to find where they already show their work: data and ML meetups such as PyData Berlin, conferences such as Berlin Buzzwords, open-source projects, university labs and AI hackathons.** Job ads alone reach mostly active job seekers, while the engineers most teams want are already employed. Sourcing through communities lets you judge talks, code and prototypes before the first interview.

## How large is the pool of tech specialists in Germany?

Germany employs more than a fifth of the EU's ICT specialists, but ML engineers are a small part of that pool. According to [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=ICT_specialists_in_employment), more than 10 million people were employed as ICT specialists across the EU in 2025. Germany accounted for 2.3 million of them, or 22.2% of the EU total.

| Indicator (Eurostat, 2025) | Figure |
|---|---|
| ICT specialists employed in the EU | more than 10 million |
| ICT specialists employed in Germany | 2.3 million |
| Germany's share of the EU total | 22.2% |
| Share of EU ICT specialists with tertiary education (of those reporting) | 68.8% |
| Share of women among EU ICT specialists | 19.5% |

Eurostat does not count ML engineers separately. The figures still show two things for hiring: most of the talent pool holds a university degree, so universities are a strong channel, and women are under-represented, so a search that relies only on existing networks tends to reproduce that imbalance.

## Which Berlin communities should you join to meet ML engineers?

Join recurring communities, because trust built over several months produces better hires than a single recruiting push. Useful options:

- **PyData Berlin.** [PyData Berlin](https://berlin.pydata.org/) organises a monthly meetup on the third Wednesday of each month, usually at different venues, plus occasional weekend hackathons and workshops and an annual conference. Hosting an evening or giving a technical talk puts your team in front of data and ML practitioners.
- **Berlin Buzzwords.** [Berlin Buzzwords](https://2027.berlinbuzzwords.de/) describes itself as a conference for modern data infrastructure, search and machine learning, focused on open-source software. The 2027 edition is scheduled for 13–15 June 2027 at the Kulturbrauerei.
- **AI and robotics meetups and reading groups.** Smaller groups focused on topics such as robot learning or LLM engineering attract specialists. See our overview of the [Berlin AI and robotics community](/articles/berlin-ai-and-robotics-community).

Show up with engineers, not only recruiters. ML engineers respond to technical conversations about real problems.

## What other channels work for sourcing ML engineers in Berlin?

Beyond meetups, five channels work well, and each suits a different role:

| Channel | Best for | What you can evaluate |
|---|---|---|
| Open-source projects and model hubs | Mid-level and senior engineers | Public code, models, datasets and review comments |
| University research groups | Junior and research roles | Theses, papers, project demos |
| Conference talks | Senior specialists | Depth of expertise and communication |
| Referrals from your own engineers | All levels | Peer judgement of working style |
| AI hackathons | Engineers who ship prototypes | How they build, prioritise and work in a team |

Open-source and hackathon channels are especially useful because the evidence is visible before you contact the person.

## Which ML specialisations should you search for separately?

Search for each ML specialisation separately, because the communities and signals differ. "Machine learning engineer" covers very different jobs:

| Specialisation | Typical work | Where they tend to show their work |
|---|---|---|
| LLM and agent engineering | Building applications and agents on language models | Open-source tools, demos, AI hackathons |
| Computer vision | Detection, segmentation, tracking on images and video | Papers, datasets, model hubs |
| Robot learning | Training policies that control physical machines | Robotics labs, physical AI hackathons, open-source robotics projects |
| MLOps and AI infrastructure | Training pipelines, serving, monitoring, compute | Infrastructure talks, open-source tooling |
| Applied research | New methods, evaluation, publications | Conferences, university groups |

Write a separate search plan for the specialisation you need. A vision engineer will rarely find your post in an LLM community, and the reverse is also true.

## How do ML roles for robotics differ from general ML roles?

ML roles for robotics need engineers who can work with sensor data, real-time constraints and hardware, which most ML engineers have not done. A recommendation-system or LLM engineer is strong in data pipelines and model training but may never have handled camera calibration, action data or a policy that controls a motor.

If your ML engineer will work on robots, adjust the search:

1. Look at robotics and physical AI events rather than general data events.
2. Ask for experience with robot datasets, simulation or deployment on edge hardware.
3. Test the hand-off from model to machine in the interview.
4. Consider pairing a strong ML engineer with a robotics engineer rather than searching for one person with both profiles.

For the infrastructure side of this work, see [AI infrastructure for robotics teams](/articles/ai-infrastructure-for-robotics-teams).

## How do you get ML engineers in Berlin to reply?

ML engineers reply to specific, technical outreach. A good first message names:

- The problem the team is solving and the data it works with.
- The model types and the training and inference stack.
- The compute available, because engineers care whether they can actually train.
- Why you contacted them in particular: a talk, a repository, a project.
- The next step, ideally a conversation with an engineer rather than a recruiter screen.

Generic messages with a list of frameworks are easy to ignore.

**HackLab is a Berlin organiser of AI × robotics (physical AI) hackathons where companies meet and recruit pre-selected builders by watching them build real prototypes in 36–48 hours.** Edition #1 (June 2026) selected 130 builders from 600+ applications and led to 5 hires at sponsor companies. Edition #2 (21–22 November 2026) selected 200 builders from 1,100+ registrations. The answers below cover what companies ask most, including what is not covered here.

## What is HackLab, in one paragraph?

HackLab runs AI × robotics hackathons at CIC Berlin. Teams of pre-selected builders spend 36–48 hours building working prototypes on real hardware, then demo them to judges and sponsor companies. The facts for both editions are in one table in [what is HackLab](/articles/what-is-hacklab).

## Why would a company work with HackLab instead of posting a job ad?

A job ad gives you CVs. HackLab shows you people building. Companies see candidates build working prototypes in 36–48 hours, inside a group that is already pre-selected.

| | Job ad | HackLab |
|---|---|---|
| What you assess | CV and interview answers | A working prototype built under time pressure |
| Pool | Whoever applies | Pre-selected (130 of 600+; 200 of 1,100+) |
| Contact | Through the application process | In person, during the build |

For the general case, see [are hackathons a good way to hire engineers](/articles/are-hackathons-a-good-way-to-hire-engineers).

## Who will we meet?

You will meet robotics, AI and ML builders. Edition #1 participants included robotics students finishing their degree, people with machine-learning backgrounds, robotics professionals across specialties, master's students building first real prototypes, and university researchers. Details are in [who attends HackLab](/articles/who-attends-hacklab).

## How are participants selected?

Participants are selected from applications. At Edition #1, HackLab selected 130 builders from 600+ applications. Edition #2 selected 200 from 1,100+ registrations. The exact selection criteria are not covered here.

## What can sponsors do?

Sponsors can take part in three ways:

1. **Set challenges** for teams to work on.
2. **Provide hardware or credits.** At Edition #2, every technology partner gives participants free credits.
3. **Meet builders in person** at the event.

How sponsorship works in general is covered in [how to sponsor a hackathon](/articles/how-to-sponsor-a-hackathon).

## Which companies have already sponsored HackLab?

Edition #1 had 14 sponsor companies, including Norma Core, Hugging Face, n8n, Lovable, Marso Robotics, Mistral AI, ITQ and Red Bull. Edition #2 has 14 partners and technology providers, including Anthropic, Hugging Face, Nebius, Cognition, Prior Labs and X Square Robot. The full lists are in [HackLab partners and sponsors](/articles/hacklab-partners-and-sponsors).

## Has anyone actually been hired?

Yes. After Edition #1, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies. Which companies hired, the roles, the cost per hire and how long the hires stayed are not covered here.

## What hardware and tracks does Edition #2 have?

Edition #2 provides robot arms, robot sets and a Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped on site. It runs four tracks:

- AI Infrastructure
- Industrial Robotics Simulation
- Autonomous Navigation
- Wildcard / Open Source

Prizes total €10,000+ (cash, hardware and more).

## Where and when are HackLab events held?

Both editions take place at CIC Berlin. Edition #1 ran on 20–21 June 2026 (36 hours). Edition #2 runs on 21–22 November 2026 (48 hours) at CIC Berlin, Lohmühlenstraße 65. Applications for Edition #2 are closed. Dates and location for a third edition are not covered here.

## Who organises HackLab?

HackLab is the organiser. Edition #2 is in collaboration with BRISA.

## How big is HackLab?

HackLab is one of the largest physical AI hackathons in Berlin: 1,100+ registrations for 200 spots (Edition #2). Across its events, HackLab has reached 1,500+ people.

## What isn't covered in this FAQ?

This FAQ does not cover the following:

- Sponsorship prices or package names
- Cost per hire, or retention of hires
- Which companies made the Edition #1 hires
- Judge names and winning teams
- Exact selection criteria
- Gender or nationality mix of participants, or attendee satisfaction
- Dates or a city for a third edition

For any of these, such as sponsorship terms, ask HackLab directly through the for-companies page.

## How HackLab helps companies hire and sponsor

HackLab gives companies in-person access to pre-selected robotics, AI and ML builders while they build working prototypes in 36–48 hours. Sponsors can set challenges, provide hardware or credits, and meet builders in person, and Edition #1 ended with 5 hires at sponsor companies. To ask about sponsorship or hiring, [partner with HackLab](/for-companies).

**Yes, companies can hire from a hackathon. After HackLab's Edition #1, the Berlin Robotics × Agentic AI Hackathon held on 20–21 June 2026, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies.** The event lasted 36 hours, drew 600+ applications and selected 130 builders. The number is small and specific, which is exactly why it is useful: it is a real outcome from a single event, not a projection.

This article looks at what that result does and does not show, and what a company can do to get a similar outcome.

## What exactly happened at HackLab Edition #1?

Edition #1 was a 36-hour building event at CIC Berlin with a selected group of builders and 14 sponsor companies. The confirmed figures from the HackLab Edition #1 Impact Report:

| Stage | Figure |
|---|---|
| Applications | 600+ |
| Builders selected after reviewing every application | 130 |
| Teams | About 20 |
| Final solutions demoed to judges and sponsor companies | Close to 20 |
| Sponsor companies | 14 |
| Participants hired by sponsor companies after the event | 5 |

Sponsors included Norma Core, Hugging Face, n8n, Lovable, Marso Robotics, Mistral AI, ITQ and Red Bull. The first edition was bootstrapped, with no main partner covering costs from day one.

## What does the number 5 actually show?

It shows that the hiring path from a hackathon to a job exists and was used. Five people moved from building at a weekend event to being hired by companies that sponsored it. Three things follow from the figures above.

### The hires came from a filtered pool

Every hire came out of a group that had already been reviewed. 600+ people applied and 130 were selected before anyone wrote a line of code. Companies were not sifting through an open crowd; they were looking at a pool that had passed a first screen.

### The hires went to companies that were present

All 5 hires were at sponsor companies. The facts do not tell us why each hire happened, but they do tell us where: inside the group of 14 companies that sponsored the event, the same group to which the final solutions were demoed.

### The evidence came from working prototypes

Close to 20 final solutions were demoed to judges and sponsor companies after 36 hours of continuous building. For any company, a demo of a working prototype is direct evidence of what a builder can do, which a CV cannot provide.

## What does the number not show?

It does not show which companies hired, for which roles, how long the process took or how the hires are doing now. HackLab has not published those details, and this article does not fill them in.

| Known | Not published |
|---|---|
| 5 participants hired by sponsor companies | Which sponsor companies hired |
| Hires came after the event | Roles, seniority, salaries |
| 130 builders selected from 600+ | Exact selection criteria |
| 14 sponsor companies in total | Retention of the people hired |

Be cautious with any article that gives a "hire rate" for hackathons in general. Outcomes depend on the event, the pool and what each company does. One event is evidence that hiring is possible, not a guarantee of a ratio.

## Who were the people available to hire?

The pool combined early-career and experienced profiles. According to HackLab, Edition #1 attendees included:

- Robotics students finishing their degree
- People with machine-learning backgrounds
- Robotics professionals across specialties
- Master's students building their first real prototypes
- University researchers

For a company, that mix means one event can feed different roles: graduate positions, research-heavy roles and experienced engineering hires. More detail is in [who attends HackLab](/articles/who-attends-hacklab).

## How can a company replicate this result?

A company can follow the same path the 5 hires took: be present, watch the work, and act on it. The steps below are what a company can do; they are not a description of what any specific Edition #1 sponsor did.

1. **Be a sponsor or partner, not a visitor.** All 5 hires were at sponsor companies. Presence gives you access to builders and demos.
2. **Name your target roles before the event.** Decide which two or three profiles you want, so your team knows whom to watch.
3. **Set a challenge or provide resources.** Sponsors can set challenges, provide hardware or credits, and meet builders in person. A challenge close to your real work attracts the people you want.
4. **Send engineers who can judge the work.** A demo is only useful if someone on your side can tell a robust solution from a lucky one.
5. **Write notes during the demos.** Close to 20 solutions is a lot to remember. One line of evidence per team is enough.
6. **Contact builders quickly after the event.** Reference what they built. The detailed sequence is in [how to turn hackathon participants into hires](/articles/how-to-turn-hackathon-participants-into-hires).

## Is one event enough to judge the hackathon channel?

One event is enough to show that the channel works, but not enough to set expectations for your own hiring numbers. Treat your first hackathon as a test: record how many builders you scored, contacted and made offers to, and compare that with your other channels.

**HackLab Edition #1, the Berlin Robotics × Agentic AI Hackathon, ran on 20–21 June 2026 at CIC Berlin. It turned 600+ applications into 130 selected builders, about 20 teams, close to 20 demoed solutions and 5 hires at sponsor companies.** The event was bootstrapped, with no main partner covering costs from day one. All Edition #1 figures come from the HackLab Edition #1 Impact Report.

## What did HackLab Edition #1 produce, in numbers?

Edition #1 produced 5 hires from a selected group of 130 builders. Read top to bottom, the table shows each stage from applications to hires.

| Stage | Result |
|---|---|
| Applications received | 600+ |
| Builders selected (every application reviewed) | 130 |
| Teams | About 20 |
| Final solutions demoed to judges and sponsors | Close to 20 |
| Sponsor companies | 14 |
| Participants hired by sponsor companies after the event | 5 |

The format was 36 hours of continuous building. At the end, teams demoed their final solutions to judges and sponsor companies.

## How were the 130 builders chosen?

HackLab reviewed every application and selected 130 builders from 600+. The exact selection criteria are not covered here.

For a company, what matters is that every builder in the room had already been through a review. Sponsors did not need to screen 600+ people. They met 130 who had passed selection.

## Who were the participants?

Edition #1 brought together five main profiles:

- Robotics students finishing their degree
- People with machine-learning backgrounds
- Robotics professionals across specialties
- Master's students building their first real prototypes
- University researchers

The mix spans early-career builders and experienced professionals. [Who attends HackLab](/articles/who-attends-hacklab) explains what each profile means for hiring.

## How could sponsors take part in Edition #1?

Sponsors were present at the point where builders showed their work. Close to 20 final solutions were demoed to judges and sponsor companies at the end of the 36 hours.

In HackLab's model, sponsors can:

- Set challenges for teams
- Provide hardware or credits
- Meet builders in person during the event

This is how a sponsor gets to judge candidates by a working prototype rather than a CV.

## What do the 5 hires show?

The 5 hires show that a 36-hour hackathon can end in actual hiring, not just brand exposure. After Edition #1, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies.

What this article does not cover:

## Why does it matter that Edition #1 was bootstrapped?

Edition #1 had no main partner covering costs from day one. Even so, it drew 600+ applications and 14 sponsor companies.

The second edition is larger. Edition #2, co-organised with BRISA, received 1,100+ registrations for 200 spots and runs for 48 hours instead of 36.

## What should companies take from Edition #1?

Edition #1 gives a company three practical signals:

1. **Demand.** 600+ people applied to build robotics and agentic AI prototypes in Berlin over one weekend.
2. **Filtering.** Only 130 were selected, after every application was reviewed.
3. **Outcome.** 5 participants went on to jobs at sponsor companies.

Companies evaluating HackLab can use these numbers as the baseline for Edition #2.

## How HackLab helps companies hire from its hackathons

At HackLab, companies watch pre-selected builders turn an idea into a working prototype within 36–48 hours, instead of judging them from a CV. Sponsors can set challenges, provide hardware or credits, and meet builders in person. Edition #1 ended with 5 hires at sponsor companies.

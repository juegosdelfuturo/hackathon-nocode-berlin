**To hire robotics engineers in Berlin, source where they already build robots: local robotics firms, university labs, the meetup scene and hands-on hackathons, then judge them on a working prototype rather than a CV.** Berlin's robotics market is made up mostly of small, software-heavy companies, so strong candidates are spread thin and rarely sit on job boards. The companies that hire fastest meet engineers in person while they are building something.

## Where are the robotics engineers in Berlin?

Most robotics engineers in Berlin work in small companies, research groups or startups, not in large corporate R&D departments. A 2025 [Prognos study for the Berlin Senate](https://www.prognos.com/en/project/ai-based-robotics-industrial-city-berlin) counted 149 companies in the Berlin metropolitan region offering (AI-based) robotics products or services. About half of them employ fewer than eleven people, and software firms make up 56% of those surveyed.

That shapes where you look:

- **Robotics vendors and startups.** Engineers here already work on perception, manipulation and autonomy. They move between small companies often and usually through personal networks.
- **Industrial users.** The same study found 85 industrial companies in the region using (AI-supported) robots in manufacturing, employing about 46,000 people. Their automation and integration engineers know production environments.
- **Universities and research labs.** TU Berlin runs a [Science of Intelligence master's track](https://www.scienceofintelligence.de/?p=17231) inside its Computer Science master's, with a curriculum that runs from robotics and control to psychology and ethics. Graduates of research groups like this are a steady source of junior and mid-level talent.
- **Community and hackathons.** Meetups and build events are where engineers from all three groups meet. See our overview of the [Berlin AI and robotics community](/articles/berlin-ai-and-robotics-community).

## What does the Berlin robotics market look like for an employer?

Berlin is a market of many small players and few large employers. The Prognos figures give the scale:

| Indicator (Berlin metropolitan region) | Figure |
|---|---|
| Companies offering (AI-based) robotics products or services | 149 |
| Jobs at those companies | about 6,000 |
| Annual sales of those companies | over €700 million |
| Industrial companies using (AI-supported) robots | 85 |
| People employed at those user companies | about 46,000 |
| Robot-using companies where AI-based robots play a role | 8% |

Two consequences follow for hiring. First, the robotics suppliers employ about 6,000 people in total, so most experienced engineers already have a job and every senior hire is likely to be a passive candidate. Second, the 8% figure shows that AI-based robotics is still early in local industry, which means engineers who combine machine learning with real hardware are rarer than either skill alone.

## Which hiring channels work best for robotics roles in Berlin?

The channels that show you a candidate's actual work outperform the ones that show you a profile. Here is how the main options compare:

| Channel | What you learn about the candidate | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Job boards and LinkedIn ads | Self-reported skills and job titles | Generalist software roles next to the robotics team |
| Agency or headhunter | A pre-screened CV and salary expectations | Senior or leadership roles with a clear spec |
| University labs and thesis supervision | Research depth and publications | Junior, research and learning-focused roles |
| Meetups and talks | Communication and interests | Building awareness over months |
| Hands-on hackathons | How they build, debug and work in a team under time pressure | Engineers who must ship working prototypes |

Most Berlin robotics teams combine two or three of these. A common pattern is employer branding through meetups, direct sourcing from labs, and hackathons for seeing candidates work on hardware.

## What process should a Berlin robotics hire follow?

A five-step process keeps robotics hiring fast without lowering the bar:

1. **Define the robotics layer.** State whether the role is mechanical and electronics, embedded and controls, perception, motion planning, or robot learning. "Robotics engineer" alone attracts the wrong applicants.
2. **Source actively.** Contact engineers in local labs and companies directly, and go to the events where they build. When the local robotics suppliers employ about 6,000 people in total, waiting for inbound applications is slow.
3. **Run one practical task.** Use a short simulation task, a debugging exercise on an existing robot stack, or observation at a hackathon. Our guide on [how to assess robotics engineers beyond the CV](/articles/how-to-assess-robotics-engineers-beyond-the-cv) covers formats.
4. **Interview on the work.** Ask the candidate to explain the trade-offs in what they built: why this sensor, why this controller, what broke.
5. **Decide within days.** In a small market, a slow process gives competing employers time to make the first offer.

## What should a robotics job ad in Berlin say?

A robotics job ad should name the robot, the stack and the problem, because engineers choose roles by the work. Include:

- The platform: arm, mobile robot, quadruped, drone or custom hardware.
- The software stack, such as ROS 2, the simulator and the learning frameworks in use.
- Whether the role touches real hardware every week or works mainly in simulation.
- The working language of the team and whether German is required.
- Who the person reports to and what they will ship in their first six months.

Keep the requirements list short. Separate the two or three skills the person must have on day one from the ones they can learn on the job.

## Should you hire international engineers for a Berlin robotics team?

Yes, if the role allows it, because the roughly 6,000 jobs at Berlin's robotics suppliers are a small pool for senior or specialised roles. Teams that work in English can recruit across Europe and beyond, and relocation to Berlin becomes part of the offer. The visa and residence steps for engineers from outside the EU are covered in [hiring international engineers in Germany](/articles/hiring-international-engineers-in-germany).

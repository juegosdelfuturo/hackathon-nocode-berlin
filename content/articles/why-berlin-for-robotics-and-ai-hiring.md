**Companies hire robotics and AI talent in Berlin because the city combines a large student population, a dense base of AI and robotics companies, and an international workforce.** Berlin had 206,458 students in winter semester 2025/26, 685 AI companies, and 149 companies supplying AI-based robotics products and services. About half of the city's startup workforce comes from abroad, which widens the pool beyond German-speaking candidates.

This article collects the numbers that matter for a hiring decision and lists the trade-offs. For the company landscape itself, see [robotics startups and companies in Berlin](/articles/robotics-startups-and-companies-in-berlin).

## How big is Berlin's talent pool for robotics and AI?

Berlin's pool is large and still growing. The [Amt für Statistik Berlin-Brandenburg](https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/173-2025) reports 206,458 students at Berlin universities in winter semester 2025/26 (preliminary figures), up 2.8% or 5,606 students on the previous year.

The share of international students rose to 26.3%, from 24.8% a year earlier. For employers, this means a steady annual flow of graduates who already live in the city, a quarter of them from abroad.

The wider population is also international. Berlin had 3,913,644 registered residents on 31 December 2025, and 24.9% of them were foreign nationals, according to the [office's register statistics](https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/presse/2026/16-einwohnerregisterstatistik).

## How many robotics and AI companies are already in Berlin?

Berlin has about 150 robotics suppliers and several hundred AI companies. A 2025 study by [Prognos for the Senate Department for Economic Affairs](https://www.prognos.com/en/project/ai-based-robotics-industrial-city-berlin) gives the most detailed picture of robotics:

- **149 companies** offer (AI-based) robotics products and services.
- They provide **about 6,000 jobs** and generate **over €700 million** in annual sales.
- About half employ **fewer than eleven people**; **56%** are software firms.
- **85 industrial companies** in the Berlin region use robots in manufacturing. They employ about 46,000 people, almost half of Berlin's industrial workforce.
- AI-based robots play a role in only **8%** of those user companies.

For AI more broadly, [ai.berlin](https://ai.berlin/ecosystem/facts), the platform run by Berlin Partner, lists 685 AI companies in Berlin, 78% of them less than ten years old. It also cites a 2025 study by the Applied AI Institute for Europe that counted 283 AI startups in Berlin, nearly a third of all German AI startups.

## Is the Berlin tech workforce international enough to hire across borders?

Yes. ai.berlin cites the German Startups Association (Bundesverband Deutsche Startups, 2025): around half of Berlin's startup workforce comes from abroad. It also cites an older 2019 figure that more than one third of AI experts in Berlin come from abroad.

In practice, this means:

1. A large share of candidates have already relocated to Germany once.
2. Teams that work in English can recruit from both the local and the international pool.
3. Hiring from outside the EU follows a defined permit process (see the trade-offs below).

## Is capital flowing into Berlin startups in 2026?

Yes, Berlin led Germany in startup funding in the first half of 2026. According to [ai.berlin's summary of the Startup Monitor 2026](https://ai.berlin/news/detail/startup-monitor-2026-what-the-figures-mean-for-berlin), Berlin startups raised about €1.69 billion in H1 2026 (EY data), up 10% year on year, across 95 funding rounds.

The same summary notes that the number of deals fell 30% year on year. Capital is concentrated in fewer, larger rounds. For hiring, funded companies compete for the same senior engineers, so planning a sourcing channel early matters.

## What are the trade-offs of hiring robotics engineers in Berlin?

Berlin's robotics sector is broad but made up of small companies. Three points follow from the data above:

- **Most local employers are small.** Half of robotics suppliers have fewer than eleven employees, so relatively few local engineers come from large hardware organisations. Plan more time for senior searches.
- **Software skills outnumber hardware skills.** 56% of robotics suppliers are software firms, so candidates with perception, ML and simulation backgrounds are easier to find than mechanical or embedded specialists.
- **Non-EU hires need a residence permit.** The [EU Blue Card](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/324659/en/) threshold in 2026 is €50,700 gross per year, or €45,934.20 for shortage occupations (including engineers and ICT specialists) and recent graduates. See [hiring international engineers in Germany](/articles/hiring-international-engineers-in-germany) for the full process.

## Where do Berlin's robotics candidates come from?

Most come from universities, research labs and the local startup scene. [Berlin's research base](https://businesslocationcenter.de/en/industry/robotik) includes TU Berlin, Humboldt-Universität, Freie Universität, the Berliner Hochschule für Technik and Fraunhofer IPK; the details are in [robotics research institutions in Berlin](/articles/robotics-research-institutions-in-berlin).

Hackathons are another visible source. HackLab's Edition #1 (20–21 June 2026, CIC Berlin) received 600+ applications for 130 places. Its profile mix included robotics students finishing their degree, people with machine-learning backgrounds, robotics professionals and university researchers.

Edition #2 (21–22 November 2026) is one of the largest physical AI hackathons in Berlin: 1,100+ registrations for 200 spots (Edition #2). Demand on that scale shows how many builders want hands-on robotics work.

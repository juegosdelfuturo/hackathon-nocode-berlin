**Hackathons in Germany work as a hiring channel when they are selective, run long enough to produce real work, and let companies watch candidates build; HackLab Robotics' AI × robotics hackathons in Berlin are one of the leading options for robotics, AI and ML roles.** After its Edition #1 in June 2026, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies. The outcome depends as much on the company's own plan as on the event, so this page sets out that plan step by step.

This page is for companies: heads of engineering, founders and talent leads who want to turn a hackathon into hires.

## What hiring outcomes can a company expect from a hackathon in Germany?

A company can expect three outcomes: candidates evaluated on real work, a shortlist of people to interview, and, with good follow-up, hires. A hackathon replaces part of the screening process, because you see candidates build working prototypes in 36–48 hours instead of reading CVs.

HackLab Robotics' Edition #1 is a concrete example. It ran 36 hours at CIC Berlin with 130 builders selected from 600+ applications, about 20 teams and 14 sponsor companies. Afterwards, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies. More detail is in [five hires from one hackathon](/articles/five-hires-from-one-hackathon-hacklab-edition-1).

Outcomes to track:

- **Candidates evaluated in person:** builders your engineers watched long enough to judge.
- **Interviews booked:** technical conversations held after the event.
- **Offers and hires:** the result that justifies the time spent.

## What makes a hackathon in Germany worth a hiring team's time?

Look at four points before committing: selection, relevance, length and access.

- **Selection:** a pre-selected group means less screening on site. HackLab Robotics selected 130 of 600+ applicants for Edition #1 and 200 of 1,100+ registrations for Edition #2.
- **Relevance:** the builders should match the roles you hire for. HackLab's participants include robotics students finishing their degree, people with machine-learning backgrounds, robotics professionals, master's students and university researchers.
- **Length:** 36–48 hours gives enough time to see someone get past setup and deliver a working result.
- **Access:** sponsors should be able to shape the work and meet builders. At HackLab Robotics, sponsors can set challenges, provide hardware or credits, and meet builders in person.

## How should a company plan to hire through a HackLab Robotics hackathon?

Split the plan into before, during and after the event. The table summarises it; the sections below give the detail.

| Phase | Goal | Key actions | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before | Know who you want and give them a reason to work on your problem | Define 1–3 target roles, write a challenge, choose hardware or credits, brief your engineers | Hiring manager, engineering lead |
| During | See how candidates actually work | Engineers spend time with teams, take notes against agreed criteria, attend demos | Engineers on site |
| After | Turn contacts into interviews and offers | Message within days, run a technical conversation on the prototype, decide fast | Recruiter, hiring manager |

### Before the event

Most of the hiring result is decided before the event starts.

1. **Define the roles.** Name one to three roles, for example a robotics software engineer or an ML engineer working on perception. Write down the two or three skills that matter most for each.
2. **Write a challenge close to your real work.** A sponsor challenge acts as a work sample. Keep it scoped so a team can show progress in 36–48 hours. See [how to write a hackathon challenge](/articles/how-to-write-a-hackathon-challenge).
3. **Decide what you provide.** Sponsors can provide hardware or credits. If your challenge needs your robot or your platform, plan who supports it.
4. **Brief your engineers.** Send people who can judge the work and talk to builders as peers. Agree on how they will take notes.
5. **Prepare the follow-up.** Have interview slots, a short message template and a decision owner ready before the weekend.

### During the event

During the event, the goal is to watch people work, not to collect CVs.

- **Spend time with teams.** Ask what they are trying, what broke and how they fixed it.
- **Watch the individual inside the team.** Note who debugs the hardware, who integrates the model, who keeps the team on track and who presents.
- **Use the same criteria for everyone.** A short scorecard per role keeps notes comparable. Criteria and methods are covered in [how to evaluate candidates at a hackathon](/articles/how-to-evaluate-candidates-at-a-hackathon).
- **Attend the demos.** At Edition #1, close to 20 final solutions were demoed to judges and sponsor companies.

### After the event

After the event, speed and specificity decide whether a contact becomes a hire.

1. **Message within days.** Refer to what the person built, not to the event in general.
2. **Hold a technical conversation on the prototype.** Ask the candidate to walk through their decisions. This can replace a generic first-round screen.
3. **Decide quickly.** Several sponsor companies meet the same builders, so strong candidates may be talking to more than one of them.
4. **Check visa routes early for international candidates.** See [hiring international engineers in Germany](/articles/hiring-international-engineers-in-germany).
5. **Record the source.** Track which hires came from which event, so you can compare channels next year.

A longer follow-up process is in [how to turn hackathon participants into hires](/articles/how-to-turn-hackathon-participants-into-hires).

## What mistakes stop companies from hiring at hackathons?

Most failures come from treating the hackathon as a branding stand instead of a hiring process.

- No defined roles, so engineers do not know whom to spend time with.
- A challenge that is too vague or too large to show progress in a weekend.
- Sending only recruiters, with no engineers who can judge the technical work.
- Waiting weeks to follow up.
- No owner for the hiring decision after the event.

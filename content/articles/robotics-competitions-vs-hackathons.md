**For hiring robotics engineers now, hackathons work better than competitions, because companies can watch each candidate build a working prototype in 36–48 hours.** Robotics competitions such as Formula Student Germany, RoboCup and the European Rover Challenge are better as long-term talent pipelines. They show what a university team engineered over months, but not who did what. Most companies get the best results by using both, with the hackathon as the point where they evaluate and hire.

## How do robotics competitions and hackathons differ?

They differ in how long teams build, who forms the team and what a company can observe. The table compares the two formats on the points that matter for hiring.

| Factor | Robotics competition | Robotics hackathon |
|---|---|---|
| Build time | A season of months, robot built before the event | 36–48 hours on site (some formats run up to 6 days) |
| Team | Fixed university or club team | Can be formed for the event |
| What judges see | The finished robot or vehicle in set tasks | Prototypes built from scratch during the event |
| What companies see | Team results, plus whoever staffs the stand or pits | Each individual's problem-solving, coding and teamwork live |
| Typical participants | University and school teams (varies by competition) | At HackLab: students, researchers and robotics professionals |
| Company role | Partner, sponsor, mentor | Challenge setter, hardware or credits provider, recruiter |
| Best use | Employer brand, long-term pipeline | Evaluating and hiring individuals |

## What can a company learn from a robotics competition?

A competition shows that a team can design, build and run a complete system to a rulebook. That is strong evidence of engineering discipline at team level.

### Formula Student Germany

[Formula Student Germany](https://www.formulastudent.de/fsg/about) "challenges university teams to design, build and race single-seater cars". It runs an all-electric competition, and self-driving technology is part of the EV class through Driverless disciplines and the optional Driverless Cup. The organisers say the event gives companies "a clear indication of the quality of the students' education" and a way to build contacts with future engineers.

### RoboCup German Open

The [RoboCup German Open 2026](https://robocup.de/) ran 10–14 March 2026 at Koelnmesse in Cologne, with more than 1,100 participants from Germany, Europe and Asia. Its organisers describe it as "größten KI-Robotik-Wettbewerb Europas" (Europe's largest AI robotics competition) and report 15% growth in university and school teams.

### European Rover Challenge

The [European Rover Challenge](https://roverchallenge.eu/) in Kraków runs teams' rovers on a Marsyard through tasks modelled on NASA missions. The 2026 edition was held 4–6 September 2026, with 124 registered teams from 29 countries. It also includes a space industry conference.

## What can a company learn from a robotics hackathon?

A hackathon shows how each person works when the problem is new and the clock is running. Companies see who debugs the hardware, who integrates the model and who leads the demo.

### HackLab (Berlin)

HackLab runs AI × robotics hackathons at CIC Berlin.

At Edition #1 (20–21 June 2026), 130 builders selected from 600+ applications formed about 20 teams and built for 36 hours. Close to 20 final solutions were demoed to judges and sponsor companies. Afterwards, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies.

Edition #2 (21–22 November 2026) runs 48 hours, with 200 builders selected from 1,100+ registrations. Teams work on robot arms, robot sets and a Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped.

### robo.innovate Hackathon (Munich)

The [robo.innovate hackathon](https://roboinnovate.mirmi.tum.de/hackathon/) sits between the two formats. It ran over six days, 23–28 October 2026, and asked teams to build working prototypes with industry hardware such as a Universal Robots cobot and a Doosan A0509. The longer format shows more depth than a weekend, while still taking place in front of partners.

### When does a competition make more sense?

A competition makes more sense when you hire graduates a year or more ahead, or need a steady brand presence at universities. Sponsoring a team or a competition keeps your name in front of students for a whole season. See [student robotics competitions in Europe](/articles/student-robotics-competitions-in-europe) for a longer list.

### When does a hackathon make more sense?

A hackathon makes more sense when you have open roles now, or need to judge skills a CV cannot show. For how to score candidates during the event, see [how to evaluate candidates at a hackathon](/articles/how-to-evaluate-candidates-at-a-hackathon).

## How can a company combine both channels?

Use competitions to build awareness and hackathons to evaluate. A practical sequence:

1. **Sponsor or mentor at a competition** to reach university teams early.
2. **Invite promising team members to a hackathon**, where you see each individual build.
3. **Follow up within days** with the builders you watched.
4. **Track hires by source** to see which channel works for which role.

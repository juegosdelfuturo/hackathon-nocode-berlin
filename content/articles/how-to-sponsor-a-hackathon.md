**To sponsor a hackathon, decide what you want from it first (hires, product adoption or technical feedback), then pick an event whose participants match that goal, agree with the organiser how you will contribute, and staff the event with engineers. The work that decides the result happens before and after the weekend: a clear challenge, a tested contribution and a follow-up plan.** The rest of this guide breaks that into steps a company can run.

## What is the first decision when sponsoring a hackathon?

The first decision is your primary goal. Everything else (which event, what you contribute, who you send) follows from it.

Most company goals fall into one of three groups:

- **Hiring:** meet engineers and see how they work before an interview.
- **Adoption:** get developers to build on your API, SDK, model or hardware.
- **Feedback and ideas:** watch outsiders use your product and see what breaks.

Pick one as primary and at most one as secondary. A sponsor that tries to do all three with two people on site usually does none of them well.

## How do you choose which hackathon to sponsor?

Choose the event whose participant profile matches your goal, not the one with the biggest audience. Ask the organiser who applies, how participants are selected and what format the event uses.

Questions worth asking before you commit:

1. **Who builds there?** Students, professionals, researchers, or a mix.
2. **Is there selection?** A reviewed application process means you meet a filtered group.
3. **How long is the build?** 24, 36 or 48 hours changes how far teams get.
4. **What does the organiser need from sponsors?** Challenges, hardware, credits, judges, mentors.
5. **What happens after?** Demos to sponsors, contact sharing rules, project repositories.

For a physical AI or robotics hiring goal, an event with real hardware on site and a selected builder pool will tell you more than a large general software event. The trade-offs between event types are covered in [software vs hardware hackathons](/articles/software-vs-hardware-hackathons).

## What can a company contribute to a hackathon?

A company can contribute in four main ways, and most sponsors combine two of them. The right mix depends on the goal you set in step one.

| Contribution | Best for goal | What you must prepare | Effort on site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor challenge | Hiring, ideas | A written brief, judging criteria, a prize or follow-up | Medium: answer questions, judge |
| Hardware | Adoption, hiring | Working, calibrated units, spares, setup docs | High: support teams all weekend |
| Cloud or API credits | Adoption | Codes or keys, quickstart, usage limits | Low to medium: unblock API issues |
| People (mentors, judges) | Hiring, brand | Named engineers, schedule | High: time on the floor |

## What should a sponsor prepare before the event?

Prepare anything a team needs to start building in the first hour. Teams lose time on missing access, not on hard problems.

A preparation checklist:

- **Challenge brief** with one problem, clear inputs and how it will be judged. See [how to write a hackathon challenge](/articles/how-to-write-a-hackathon-challenge).
- **Access:** credit codes, API keys or accounts created and tested before day one.
- **Hardware:** units charged, calibrated and labelled, with a one-page setup guide.
- **Documentation:** a quickstart that runs end to end in under 15 minutes on a fresh laptop.
- **Staff rota:** who is on the floor at each hour, including overnight if the event runs continuously.
- **Hiring path:** a named contact and a simple next step (a call, a take-home, an on-site day).

## Who should a company send to a sponsored hackathon?

Send engineers who know your product well enough to fix a team's problem in five minutes, plus one person who owns the business goal. Builders judge a company by the quality of help they get at 2 a.m.

A practical staffing pattern for a two-day event:

| Role | Number | Job during the event |
|---|---|---|
| Technical mentor | 2–3 | Unblock teams on your API or hardware, review code |
| Hiring or partnerships lead | 1 | Note strong builders, book follow-up calls |
| Judge (if offered) | 1 | Score demos against the published criteria |

Keep the team consistent across the event. Builders remember the engineer who helped them, and that person is the best first contact after the event.

## What happens after the hackathon ends?

The follow-up is where most of the value is captured or lost. Contact the people you noted within days, while their project is still fresh.

A simple follow-up sequence:

1. **Day 1–3:** message the builders you flagged, referencing their specific project.
2. **Week 1:** hold short calls with the strongest candidates or teams using your product.
3. **Week 2:** move hiring candidates into your normal process, with the hackathon work as evidence.
4. **Week 4:** review results against the goal you set before the event.

Use the same metrics you chose at the start. The method is in [how to measure hackathon sponsorship ROI](/articles/how-to-measure-hackathon-sponsorship-roi).

## What does a sponsor timeline look like?

A sponsor timeline has four phases, and the weekend itself is only one of them. Treat it as a short project with an owner.

| Phase | Main tasks | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Decide | Set goal, shortlist events, talk to organisers | Engineering or DevRel lead |
| Prepare | Brief, access, hardware, staff rota | Technical mentor |
| Event | Mentor, judge, note builders | Whole on-site team |
| Follow up | Contact builders, hire, measure | Hiring or partnerships lead |

**To evaluate engineers at a hackathon, fix five or six criteria before the event, score each person (not only each team) on a 1–4 scale at several points during building, write one line of evidence for every score, and calibrate with colleagues before contacting anyone.** The final demo is only one data point. Most of the useful evidence appears in the hours of building before it.

This article gives you a rubric, an observation schedule and the rules that keep scores fair.

## Why do you need a rubric at a hackathon?

Without a rubric, observers reward whoever presents best. A hackathon produces a lot of impressions in a short time, and the loudest demo tends to win the debate afterwards.

Structure fixes this. Google's re:Work guide on [structured interviewing](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices) describes four elements: role-relevant questions, scoring with standardized rubrics, recording feedback so evaluators can review it, and interviewer training and calibration. The guide states that "structured interviews result in increased predictive validity and decreased differences between demographic groups."

A hackathon is not an interview, but the same elements apply: fixed criteria, a shared scale, written evidence and calibration.

## Which criteria should you score?

Score criteria that match the role you are hiring for. This rubric works for most robotics, AI and ML engineering roles; drop or add rows to fit yours.

| Criterion | What to observe | Score 1 | Score 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem framing | How they turned the challenge into a buildable scope | Starts coding without a plan | Defines a narrow goal and a test for success |
| Technical depth | Quality of the core work they own | Copies examples without adapting them | Explains trade-offs and why the approach works |
| Debugging | Response when hardware, models or code fail | Waits or guesses | Isolates the fault and tests one change at a time |
| Collaboration | How work is split and shared | Works alone, blocks others | Unblocks teammates, makes interfaces clear |
| Prioritisation | What they cut as time runs out | Keeps adding features | Cuts scope to ship a working core |
| Communication | Demo and answers to questions | Cannot explain their own part | Explains limits honestly and answers directly |

Write each anchor before the event. For a fuller list of role skills, see [skills to look for when hiring robotics engineers](/articles/skills-to-look-for-when-hiring-robotics-engineers).

## When during the event should you observe candidates?

Observe at four fixed points, so every team is seen at the same stages. A schedule for a 36- or 48-hour event:

1. **Planning (first hours):** note how teams scope the problem and who drives the plan. Score problem framing.
2. **First integration (around the middle):** this is when parts are connected and things break. Score debugging and collaboration.
3. **Late stage (final third):** watch what teams cut. Score prioritisation.
4. **Demo and Q&A:** ask each person one question about their own part. Score communication and technical depth.

Rotate observers between teams at each point, so no team is scored by a single person.

## How do you score individuals inside a team?

Ask directly and verify by watching. Teams share a result, but you hire one person.

- **Ask "which part did you build?"** during a quiet moment, then look at that part in the demo.
- **Watch the terminal or the robot, not the slides.** The person fixing the gripper at hour 30 is easy to identify if you are in the room.
- **Note helpful behaviour that does not show in the demo,** such as the person who set up the shared environment or explained ROS to two teammates.
- **Do not penalise the quiet teammate** for not presenting. Score communication from your one-to-one question, not from who held the microphone.

## How should you adapt the rubric to the hackathon track?

Weight criteria by what each track tests. HackLab's Edition #2, for example, has four tracks with different demands:

| Track | Weight more heavily |
|---|---|
| AI Infrastructure | Technical depth in serving, data pipelines and tooling |
| Industrial Robotics Simulation | Problem framing and the link between simulation and the real task |
| Autonomous Navigation | Debugging on real hardware and sensor data |
| Wildcard / Open Source | Prioritisation and communication, since scope is open |

Hardware on site at Edition #2 includes robot arms, robot sets and a Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped. The track details are explained in [HackLab Edition #2 tracks explained](/articles/hacklab-edition-2-tracks-explained).

## How do you keep the evaluation fair?

Score only job-related behaviour you observed, and leave everything else out. Three rules:

- **Evidence or no score.** If you did not see it, leave the cell empty rather than guessing.
- **Calibrate the same evening.** Compare scores across observers, discuss any gap of two points or more, and agree on a final score with evidence.
- **Keep protected characteristics out.** In Germany, the [General Equal Treatment Act (AGG)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_agg/englisch_agg.html) aims to prevent discrimination "on the grounds of race or ethnic origin, gender, religion or belief, disability, age or sexual orientation," and it covers selection criteria for access to employment. None of these belongs on a scoring sheet.

A hackathon also favours people who can spend a full weekend at an event. Keep that in mind when you compare hackathon candidates with people from other channels.

## What should you do with the scores?

Use them to decide whom to contact and what to talk about. The scores are the start of the hiring conversation, not an offer.

- Shortlist people with high scores on the criteria that matter most for the role.
- Use the evidence lines to personalise your first message.
- Skip repeating what you already saw. Your next step should test what the hackathon could not, as covered in [how to assess robotics engineers beyond the CV](/articles/how-to-assess-robotics-engineers-beyond-the-cv).

**Measure hackathon sponsorship ROI by choosing one primary goal and its metric before the event, counting the full cost (including the hours your engineers spend), and tracking outcomes for a fixed window of about 90 days.** Return is then the value of the outcome (hires, active users, fixed issues) minus the full cost, divided by the full cost. Set the metric before the event: chosen afterwards, it tends to drift towards whatever numbers happen to look good.

## Why is hackathon ROI hard to measure?

It is hard because the main outcomes (hires and long-term product adoption) arrive weeks after the event and outside the event's own reporting. Logo impressions and booth visits are easy to count but rarely tied to a business result.

Three common gaps:

- **No baseline:** the company does not know its normal cost or time to hire for the role.
- **No attribution:** candidates met at the event enter the pipeline without a source tag.
- **Missing costs:** staff time and hardware are left out, so the cost looks lower than it was.

Fixing these three gaps is most of the work.

## Which metrics should a sponsor track for each goal?

Track one funnel per goal, from first contact to business result. Each stage should be countable from a list you control, not from the organiser's estimates.

| Goal | Leading metric (week 1–2) | Outcome metric (day 90) | How to capture it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hiring | Builders flagged, follow-up calls booked | Interviews, offers, hires | Tag source "hackathon-[event]" in your applicant tracking system |
| Product adoption | Teams using your product during the event | Accounts still active, usage volume | Unique credit codes or keys per team |
| Feedback | Issues logged during the event | Issues fixed and shipped | One shared issue board for the event |
| Ideas | Prototypes built on your challenge | Ideas taken into the roadmap or a follow-up project | Short review after demos |
| Employer brand | Builders who joined your talks or table | Inbound applications citing the event | "How did you hear about us" field |

Pick the metric before you sign. If the goal is hiring, the number of people who visited a booth is not the metric.

## How do you calculate hackathon sponsorship ROI?

Use a standard ROI formula with the full cost on the bottom line. The value side needs an internal estimate that your finance or hiring team agrees with in advance.

**ROI = (Value of outcomes − Full cost) ÷ Full cost**

Where:

- **Full cost** = sponsorship contribution + staff hours × internal hourly cost + hardware (depreciation, damage, shipping) + credits issued + travel + swag and prizes you fund.
- **Value of outcomes** for hiring = number of hires × the cost you would otherwise spend per hire for that role (agency fees, job ads, interview hours).
- **Value of outcomes** for adoption = retained accounts × your estimated value per retained developer account.

Write your internal per-hire and per-account values into the plan before the event. Agreeing them afterwards invites bias.

## What does a 90-day tracking sheet look like?

A tracking sheet needs one row per person or team you met and a few dated columns. Keep it shared between the on-site staff and the hiring or DevRel owner.

| Column | Example entry | Filled by |
|---|---|---|
| Name / team | Team building a pick-and-place demo | On-site mentor |
| What they built | Project link, track, your product used | On-site mentor |
| Strength observed | Fixed a calibration issue alone at hour 20 | On-site mentor |
| Next step and date | Call booked for day 4 | Hiring lead |
| Stage at day 14 | Technical interview | Hiring lead |
| Stage at day 90 | Hired / not hired / still in process | Hiring lead |

The "strength observed" column is the most valuable: it records evidence you cannot get from a CV. For more on what to observe, see [how to evaluate candidates at a hackathon](/articles/how-to-evaluate-candidates-at-a-hackathon).

## What do published sponsor results look like?

Few organisers publish sponsor outcomes, so use the ones that exist as context, not as targets. Two examples report different outcome types.

- **Hiring outcome:** HackLab Edition #1 (Berlin, 20–21 June 2026) selected 130 builders from 600+ applications; close to 20 final solutions were demoed and 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies after the event. The breakdown is in [five hires from one hackathon](/articles/five-hires-from-one-hackathon-hacklab-edition-1).
- **Adoption outcome:** [Major League Hacking's sponsor page](https://sponsor.mlh.com/) reports a case study in which MLH promoted Twilio at 170 events in the 2023 hackathon season, producing 585 project submissions and 2,000+ developers using Twilio products.

The first is a hiring metric at a single selective event; the second is an adoption metric across a season of events. Neither is a benchmark for your company, because your role, product and follow-up differ.

## Who should own the ROI measurement inside the company?

One person should own the measurement from the day the sponsorship is signed until the day-90 review. Usually that is the hiring lead for a hiring goal and the DevRel or product lead for an adoption goal.

The owner has three jobs:

- **Before:** write the goal, the metric, the per-hire or per-account value and the cost estimate into one short plan.
- **During:** make sure on-site staff fill in the tracking sheet every evening, not from memory a week later.
- **After:** run the day-14 and day-90 reviews and report the result against the plan, including if it fell short.

Without a named owner, the tracking sheet usually stops being updated once the event team returns to normal work.

## Which mistakes distort hackathon ROI?

The biggest distortions come from counting the wrong things or stopping too early. Avoid these:

1. **Measuring at day 7** and calling hiring a failure before interviews finish.
2. **Leaving staff time out** of the cost, which inflates ROI.
3. **Counting codes handed out** instead of codes actually used.
4. **No source tag** in the applicant system, so hires from the event go uncounted.
5. **Changing the goal** after the event to whatever looked best.

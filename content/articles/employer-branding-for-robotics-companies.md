**Robotics and AI companies build employer brand with engineers by letting them experience the work, not by describing the culture.** That means publishing tools, explaining real technical problems, putting hardware and APIs in builders' hands at events, and making your own engineers visible. Engineers judge a company by what its engineers build and how they talk about it.

This article covers where engineers form their view of an employer and which actions move it.

## Where do engineers form their opinion of a company?

Mostly through other engineers. In a 2022 Stack Overflow Knows survey of 2,600+ tech professionals, developers [most frequently discovered companies they might want to work for through word of mouth](https://stackoverflow.blog/2022/12/15/job-insights-from-the-tech-community-the-latest-survey-results-from-stack-overflow-knows/), with 46% using their personal network.

The same survey found that a focus on the developer experience (42%) and the product or solution the company sells (35%) made a company more appealing.

For a robotics company, that points to two levers: what your engineers say to their peers, and how interesting your technical problems look from outside.

## What do engineers want to see from an employer?

They want evidence of hard, real problems and of trust. In the [Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work), the three top-ranked factors for job satisfaction were:

1. Autonomy and trust to manage your own tasks
2. Competitive pay and benefits
3. Solving real-world problems

Robotics has an advantage on the third point. Your robots move in the physical world, and that is easy to show. The task is to show it in a way engineers find credible.

## Which employer branding activities work for robotics and AI companies?

The activities that work let engineers see or touch the work. Compare the common options:

| Activity | What engineers see | Effort | Signal strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Careers page with values | Claims about culture | Low | Weak on its own |
| Engineering blog posts | How your team solves specific problems | Medium | Strong if technical and specific |
| Open-source code or datasets | The quality of your actual work | Medium to high | Strong |
| Conference talks by engineers | Who works there and what they know | Medium | Strong |
| Hackathon challenge with your API or hardware | What it feels like to build with your tools | Medium | Strong, and direct contact |
| Booth or banner only | Your logo | Low | Weak |

The pattern is consistent: the closer the activity is to real engineering work, the more it says about you.

## How does open source help a robotics employer brand?

Open source lets engineers use your work before they ever talk to you. Hugging Face, a sponsor of HackLab's Edition #1 and a partner of Edition #2, maintains [LeRobot](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot), described in its repository as "Making AI for Robotics more accessible with end-to-end learning," with models, datasets and tools for real-world robotics in PyTorch.

You do not need a project of that scope. Options for smaller teams:

- Release a driver, a simulation environment or a dataset your team already maintains.
- Publish example code for your hardware or API.
- Contribute fixes to open-source tools you depend on, under your company's name.

For how teams use these tools in practice, see [open-source robotics with LeRobot and Hugging Face](/articles/open-source-robotics-lerobot-and-hugging-face).

## How do hackathons build employer brand?

A hackathon puts your tools and engineers in front of builders for a full weekend. Builders spend hours with your tools and your people while they are actively building, instead of seeing your name for a few seconds.

What makes the difference:

- **Bring something teams can use.** Hardware, credits or a dataset that solves a real problem for teams gets used and remembered. Practical advice is in [providing hardware to a robotics hackathon](/articles/providing-hardware-to-a-robotics-hackathon).
- **Send engineers, not only brand staff.** Builders want to ask technical questions and get real answers.
- **Set a challenge that reflects your real work.** It shows what a job with you involves.
- **Be honest about limits.** If your API breaks at 3 a.m., the engineer who fixes it on site builds more trust than a polished slide.

At HackLab's Edition #2, partners and technology providers include X Square Robot, Hugging Face, Anthropic, Cognition, Nebius and Seeed Studio, and on-site hardware includes robot arms, robot sets and a Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped.

## Who should own employer branding with engineers?

Engineering should own the content; recruiting or marketing should own the schedule. If only the brand team owns it, the output drifts towards slogans. If nobody owns the schedule, posts and talks stop after the first quarter.

A split that works for small and mid-sized teams:

- **Engineers:** write posts, give talks, maintain open-source releases and mentor at events.
- **Recruiting:** decide which roles the activity supports and track inbound candidates.
- **Marketing or DevRel:** edit, publish and plan the calendar.

Give engineers time for this in their planning, or it will always lose to the next sprint.

## How do you measure employer brand among engineers?

Measure behaviour, not impressions. Useful indicators:

- Inbound applications that mention a talk, post, repository or event
- Candidates who already used your tools before applying
- Offer acceptance rate for engineering roles
- Referrals from your own engineers
- Builders at events who ask for a follow-up conversation

Track them before and after each activity. If none of them move, change the activity.

## What should robotics companies avoid in employer branding?

Avoid claims without evidence. The common errors:

- Generic values on a careers page with no example of the work behind them.
- Showing robot videos with no explanation of the engineering problem.
- Sending only marketing staff to technical events.
- Sponsoring an event and leaving no way for builders to use your tools.

Engineers talk to each other, so an event where your company was invisible or unhelpful also shapes your brand. The local scene is described in [Berlin's AI and robotics community](/articles/berlin-ai-and-robotics-community).

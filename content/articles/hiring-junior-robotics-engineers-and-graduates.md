**To hire junior robotics engineers and graduates, recruit where students build real systems (thesis projects, competitions and hackathons), assess fundamentals and learning speed rather than tool lists, and plan the mentoring before you make the offer.** Graduate CVs look very similar, so the projects a candidate has built are the best way to tell them apart. Companies that meet students before graduation hire them before the wider market does.

## How many engineering graduates does Germany produce?

Germany produces a large graduate pool every year. According to [Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/10/PD26_352_213.html), about 527,600 students and doctoral candidates earned a university degree in the 2025 exam year, 3% (16,000) more than in 2024. One in four degrees (25%) was in engineering.

Robotics is one specialism within that pool, and degree titles rarely show how much hands-on robot work a graduate has done. That is why the projects a graduate has completed outside the lecture hall carry so much weight.

## Why hire junior robotics engineers at all?

Junior hires build a team's long-term capacity at a lower cost than senior hires. They also learn your stack from the start, without habits from a different robot platform. Germany's research network is investing in this pipeline: the Robotics Institute Germany, funded with [€20 million by the Federal Ministry of Education and Research](https://www.tum.de/en/news-and-events/all-news/press-releases/details/robotics-institute-germany-established), lists "promoting talent and offering training and education" among its goals, including English-taught master's programmes and a robotics doctoral programme.

The trade-off is mentoring time. A junior engineer needs a senior engineer who reviews their code and explains hardware decisions, so plan that capacity before you hire.

## Where do you find robotics graduates?

Find them in the places where students build complete systems:

| Channel | When to engage | What you learn |
|---|---|---|
| Master's thesis supervision | 6–12 months before graduation | Depth on one problem, research skills |
| Internships and working-student roles | During studies | Day-to-day work with your team and hardware |
| Student robotics competitions | During the competition season | Teamwork, integration, reliability under pressure |
| University lab open days and career fairs | Once or twice a year | Awareness and first contact |
| Hands-on hackathons | Around each event | Building speed, teamwork, learning under time pressure |

The strongest pipelines combine at least two channels, for example offering thesis topics and meeting students at hackathons. For competition-based pipelines, see [student robotics competitions in Europe](/articles/student-robotics-competitions-in-europe).

## What should you assess in a junior robotics engineer?

Assess fundamentals and learning ability first, because tools can be taught in weeks. A junior assessment should cover:

1. **Programming.** Can they write clean Python or C++ and explain it?
2. **Math basics.** Coordinate transforms, kinematics and simple control.
3. **One real project.** Something that runs on hardware or in a simulator, built mostly by them.
4. **Debugging attitude.** How they approached the last thing that did not work.
5. **Learning speed.** How quickly they picked up a new tool or framework, with an example.

Keep the practical task small and clearly scoped. Juniors with less interview experience perform better when they know what is expected.

## Should you start with internships, thesis projects or full-time offers?

Start with the format that matches your team's capacity to mentor. Each option has a different cost and a different level of commitment:

- **Thesis project.** Lowest cost for the company and a long evaluation period. It works best when the thesis topic is close to a real problem your team needs solved.
- **Internship or working-student role.** The student works with your stack and team for several months, so both sides know each other well before a full-time offer.
- **Direct full-time offer.** Fastest, and necessary when a graduate already has other offers. It works when you have seen the candidate's work in another setting, such as a hackathon or a competition.

Many teams use thesis projects and internships as a pipeline and make full-time offers to the students who performed best. Whatever the format, give the student a real problem and a named contact. A project that never touches the real robot teaches neither side much.

## How do you hire international graduates in Germany?

International graduates of German universities have two routes that make hiring them easier. First, according to the [City of Munich](https://stadt.muenchen.de/service/en-GB/info/residence-permit-job-search/10400882/n0), graduates who studied in Germany on a study permit can stay for up to 18 months after their degree to look for a job. Once they have a suitable job offer, they can apply for a residence permit for employment.

Second, the EU Blue Card has a lower salary threshold for recent graduates. The [City of Stuttgart Welcome Center](https://www.stuttgart.de/en/microsite/welcome-center/ankommen/aufenthalt/blaue-karte-eu) lists a reduced threshold of €45,934.20 gross per year in 2026 for graduates who finished their degree within the last three years, compared with the general threshold of €50,700.

| Route (2026) | Key condition |
|---|---|
| Job-search residence permit | Up to 18 months after graduating from a German university |
| EU Blue Card, reduced threshold | €45,934.20 gross per year for graduates of the last three years |
| EU Blue Card, general threshold | €50,700 gross per year |

Salary thresholds are updated regularly, so confirm details with the local immigration office before making an offer.

## How do you onboard and keep junior robotics engineers?

Give juniors a defined first project and a named mentor. A simple 90-day plan works:

- **Weeks 1–2:** Set up the development environment, run the robot stack and complete safety training.
- **Weeks 3–6:** Fix small, real issues with code review from the mentor.
- **Weeks 7–12:** Own one small component end to end and demo it to the team.

Juniors stay when they can see their growth path: which skills lead to more ownership, and when that is reviewed.

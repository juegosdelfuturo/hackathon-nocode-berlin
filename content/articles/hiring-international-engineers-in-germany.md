**To hire an engineer from outside the EU in Germany, most companies use the EU Blue Card, which in 2026 requires a gross salary of at least €50,700 per year, or €45,934.20 for engineers, ICT specialists and recent graduates.** The candidate needs a recognised degree (or, for IT roles, three years of experience) and a job offer of at least six months. Employers in Berlin can speed up the process with the accelerated skilled worker procedure, which the city estimates at about 15 weeks to entry.

All figures below come from Berlin's official immigration pages (Landesamt für Einwanderung, LEA). Rules may change; check the linked pages before you make an offer.

## What are the EU Blue Card salary thresholds in 2026?

There are two thresholds. Berlin's [EU Blue Card service page](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/324659/en/) lists them for 2026:

| Case | Monthly gross | Annual gross |
|---|---|---|
| General threshold | €4,225.00 | €50,700.00 |
| Shortage occupations (engineers, ICT, natural scientists, mathematicians, physicians and others) | €3,827.85 | €45,934.20 |
| Any profession, degree completed no more than three years before applying | €3,827.85 | €45,934.20 |

Two details matter for offer letters:

- **Variable pay does not count.** Success bonuses and other fully variable payments are excluded from the threshold.
- **Federal Employment Agency approval** is needed if the salary is below €4,225.00 gross per month. Above that, no approval step is required.

For a robotics or ML engineer, the shortage-occupation threshold usually applies, because engineers and ICT-qualified workers are on the list.

## Which candidates qualify for an EU Blue Card?

Candidates qualify through a degree or, for IT roles, through experience. The Berlin page lists these requirements:

1. **Qualification:** a German degree, a recognised foreign degree, or a comparable foreign degree. Foreign degrees are checked in the anabin database; unlisted degrees may need a separate certificate assessment.
2. **IT route without a degree:** ICT managers and specialists (ISCO-08 groups 133 or 25) need at least three years of relevant experience within the last seven years.
3. **Job offer:** an employment contract or concrete offer of at least six months, with an employer that has a place of business in Germany.
4. **Health insurance:** German statutory insurance or comparable private cover.

## How long is the Blue Card valid, and can holders change jobs?

The card is issued for up to four years. With a fixed-term contract it normally lasts three months beyond the contract's end.

After one year in the job, the holder may take any employment without notifying the immigration authority or changing the card, according to the Berlin page.

## What is the fast-track procedure, and when should employers use it?

The accelerated skilled worker procedure (beschleunigtes Fachkräfteverfahren, § 81a Residence Act) is employer-initiated and coordinates every authority involved. The [LEA's procedure page](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/service/business-immigration-service/beschleunigtes-fachkraefte-verfahren/) sets these target timelines:

| Step | Target time |
|---|---|
| Recognition of qualifications | about 8 weeks (once, with justification, extendable) |
| Federal Employment Agency approval | within 1 week |
| Visa appointment at the German mission | within 3 weeks |
| Visa issuance after document check | within a further 3 weeks |
| **Total until entry** | **about 15 weeks, if no delays** |

Conditions for using it:

- The candidate is still abroad and has not yet applied for a visa.
- A contract, or at least a draft, exists and the job is at a Berlin workplace.
- The candidate gives the employer a power of attorney.
- The employer signs a written agreement with the Business Immigration Service.

The fee is **€411**, listed on the [service page](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/329325/). Costs for certified translations and recognition are extra and paid by the worker or employer. Spouses and children can be included if their entry is possible within six months of the worker's.

## What does Berlin's Business Immigration Service do for employers?

The [Business Immigration Service](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/service/business-immigration-service/) (BIS) is a single point of contact for companies, run by the LEA with IHK Berlin and Berlin Partner. Registration is free and required before any case is processed. It works by appointment only.

The BIS:

- gives initial advice on residence and work permits,
- issues and extends residence permits for employment, including the EU Blue Card,
- issues ICT cards for intra-company transfers,
- runs the fast-track procedure for employers with a Berlin establishment.

## What does a Blue Card cost, and how long does issuance take?

Issuance fees are €100 or less. The Berlin page lists €100 for an initial electronic residence permit (eAT) or €56 for a sticker label, with the eAT ready for collection in 4 to 6 weeks. Applications are online only.

## When can a Blue Card holder get permanent residence?

Blue Card holders can apply for a settlement permit after 21 or 27 months. Berlin's [settlement permit page](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/326556/en/) sets:

- **27 months** of qualifying employment with basic German (CEFR A1), or
- **21 months** with sufficient German (CEFR B1),
- with statutory pension contributions paid for the whole period.

Supporting German lessons can shorten the path to permanent residence by six months, which is a retention argument for employers.

## What are the steps to hire a non-EU robotics engineer in Berlin?

1. Confirm the role fits a shortage occupation and set the fixed salary above the threshold.
2. Check the candidate's degree in anabin, or document three years of IT experience.
3. Register your company with the BIS (free).
4. If the candidate is abroad, sign the fast-track agreement and pay the €411 fee.
5. Send a contract or draft contract and collect the power of attorney.
6. Plan about 15 weeks to entry, then book the Blue Card appointment after arrival.

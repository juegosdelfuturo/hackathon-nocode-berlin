**A job fair is a reach channel: you meet many candidates briefly and collect applications. A hackathon is an evidence channel: you meet fewer people, but you watch them build working software or robots for 24 to 48 hours.** For specialist engineering roles, the hackathon usually produces better signal per candidate. For volume and early-career hiring, the job fair is usually more efficient.

Most companies hiring engineers benefit from knowing exactly which problem each channel solves.

## What is the main difference between a hackathon and a job fair?

The difference is what you can observe. At a job fair you see a CV and a short conversation. At a hackathon you see a person define a problem, write code, debug and present a result under time pressure.

That changes the starting point of your process. After a job fair you have leads to screen. After a hackathon you already have the equivalent of a long technical exercise for every person you watched.

## How do hackathons and job fairs compare side by side?

The two channels differ on almost every dimension that matters to a hiring team:

| Dimension | Job fair | Hackathon |
|---|---|---|
| What you see | CV, short conversation | Working prototype, process, teamwork |
| Time per candidate | A few minutes | Hours of observation |
| Number of candidates met | High | Lower, depends on event size |
| Who attends | Candidates who chose to visit employers | Builders who chose to spend a weekend building |
| Who from your company attends | Recruiters, some engineers | Mainly engineers, plus one recruiter |
| Main output | Applications and contacts | Scored shortlist with evidence |
| Best for | Graduates, volume roles, brand awareness | Specialist and senior technical roles |
| Preparation | Stand, materials, job list | Challenge brief, resources, rubric |

Neither column is "better" in general. They answer different questions.

## When is a job fair the better choice?

A job fair is the better choice when you need many applicants for similar roles. Typical cases:

- You run a graduate programme or hire several interns at once.
- You hire across functions, including non-technical roles.
- Your company is unknown to students in a region and you need basic awareness.
- You want to explain career paths to people who are still choosing a specialisation.

Job fairs also suit candidates who would not enter a 36-hour building event, for example people with caring duties or those early in their studies. For graduate pipelines in robotics, see [hiring junior robotics engineers and graduates](/articles/hiring-junior-robotics-engineers-and-graduates).

## When is a hackathon the better choice?

A hackathon is the better choice when a wrong technical hire is expensive and CVs say little. Typical cases:

- **Specialist roles** such as robot learning, simulation, autonomous navigation or ML infrastructure.
- **Hardware-heavy roles,** where you need to see someone work with real robots, sensors and failures.
- **Passive talent,** people who are not browsing job boards but will spend a weekend building.
- **Developer adoption,** when you also want builders to try your API, model or hardware.

## Which channel tells candidates more about your company?

A hackathon tells candidates more, because your engineers work next to them. That matters: in a 2022 Stack Overflow Knows survey of 2,600+ tech professionals, [19% said they had stopped pursuing a job](https://stackoverflow.blog/2022/12/15/job-insights-from-the-tech-community-the-latest-survey-results-from-stack-overflow-knows/) because they could not find enough information about what working at the company was like.

At a job fair, candidates learn what your recruiters say about the team. At a hackathon, they learn how your engineers explain a problem, how they handle a bug at 2 a.m. and how they treat a junior teammate. That is the information that was missing for those respondents.

The reverse also holds. A team that sends no engineers, or sends people who only pitch, shows candidates something too.

## How should a company split effort between the two?

Match the channel to the role, then measure each separately. A simple allocation:

1. **List your open roles for the next 6–12 months** and mark each as "volume" or "specialist".
2. **Use job fairs and campus events for volume roles,** and judge them by applications and interviews generated.
3. **Use one or two selective hackathons for specialist roles,** and judge them by shortlisted builders, offers and accepted offers.
4. **Do not compare them on cost per contact.** A hackathon produces fewer contacts by design; compare offers and accepted offers instead.
5. **Review after each event** and move effort to the channel that produces accepted offers for each role type.

## Can you combine a job fair approach with a hackathon?

Yes, by keeping the hackathon focused on building and doing the job-fair work around it. Practical ways:

- Share a one-page overview of your team and open roles in the event channel instead of running a stand.
- Hold short office hours with your engineers at fixed times, so builders choose when to talk.
- Invite strong builders to a follow-up visit at your office, which works like a small private job fair.

This keeps building time intact while still giving candidates the information they would get at a fair.

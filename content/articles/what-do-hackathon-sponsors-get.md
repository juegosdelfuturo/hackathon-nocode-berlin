**Hackathon sponsors get four things: time with engineers while they build, hard evidence of how those engineers work, real-world use of the sponsor's product, and visibility with a technical audience that ignores most advertising.** How much of each you get depends on what you contribute and how many of your own engineers you send. Below, the benefits are split by the team inside your company that receives them.

## What does the hiring team get from sponsoring a hackathon?

The hiring team gets to see candidates do the job before the first interview. Instead of reading a CV, recruiters and engineering managers watch a person scope a problem, debug under time pressure and present a result.

Concrete hiring benefits:

- **Work samples:** a prototype, a repository and a demo for every builder you meet.
- **Collaboration signal:** how someone behaves in a team at hour 30, not in a 45-minute interview.
- **A pre-filtered pool:** at events with application review, the organiser has already screened applicants.
- **Warm first contact:** builders respond faster to an engineer who helped them than to a cold message.

At HackLab Edition #1, 130 builders were selected from 600+ applications, and 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies after the event. For how to run the hiring side end to end, see [how to turn hackathon participants into hires](/articles/how-to-turn-hackathon-participants-into-hires).

## What does the product or DevRel team get?

The product team gets dozens of people using its API, SDK, model or hardware intensively for one or two days. That produces usage and feedback that are hard to get any other way.

What this looks like in practice:

- **Onboarding test:** you see exactly where new users get stuck in your docs or setup.
- **Unexpected use cases:** teams combine your product with others in ways your roadmap did not plan.
- **Bug reports with context:** issues found while building something real, often with reproducible code.
- **Early advocates:** builders who shipped something with your product are likely to reuse it.

This benefit is strongest when you provide something teams must touch, such as credits or hardware. The credits side is covered in detail in [providing API credits to hackathon teams](/articles/providing-api-credits-to-hackathon-teams).

## What does the brand or employer-branding team get?

The brand team gets visibility with engineers in a setting where the company is useful, not just present. Logo placement is the smallest part of it.

Visibility usually comes through:

- Event pages, opening talks and challenge announcements.
- Your challenge or hardware being named in team demos.
- Projects that participants share publicly afterwards.
- The reputation your mentors build on the floor.

Builders talk to each other during and after the event, and the companies that helped them come up in those conversations. A sponsor whose engineers solve problems on the floor gives builders a concrete reason to remember the company; a larger banner does not.

For a startup, this visibility can be the main return, because it reaches engineers the company could not afford to reach with advertising. For a larger company, it supports hiring by making the company known as a place where engineers build real systems. Both are covered in [employer branding for robotics companies](/articles/employer-branding-for-robotics-companies).

## Which benefit comes from which contribution?

Each benefit is tied to a specific contribution. If you want a benefit, you need to fund the contribution that produces it.

| Sponsor goal | Contribution that delivers it | What you get back |
|---|---|---|
| Hire engineers | Meet builders in person; set a challenge | Shortlist with work samples and demos |
| Grow product adoption | Provide credits or hardware | Teams building on your product, usage data |
| Improve docs and onboarding | Provide credits; mentor on the floor | Live list of setup and API friction |
| Source ideas | Set a challenge | Prototypes that attack your problem from new angles |
| Employer brand | Send engineers; judge demos | Reputation with a technical audience |

These map to the three sponsor options HackLab offers: setting challenges, providing hardware or credits, and meeting builders in person.

## What do sponsors not get from a hackathon?

Sponsors do not get production-ready software, guaranteed hires or automatic rights to participants' work. Setting expectations on these three points avoids disappointment inside your company.

- **Prototypes, not products:** 36–48 hours yields a working demo, not hardened code.
- **No guaranteed hires:** hires come from follow-up, not from attendance alone.
- **No assumed ownership:** who owns a project is set by the event rules; check them and agree anything extra in writing.
- **No passive results:** a logo with no staff on site produces little measurable return.

## How do sponsor benefits differ between software and robotics hackathons?

At robotics and physical AI hackathons, sponsors see skills that a software event cannot show: integrating sensors, controlling real hardware and making a model work outside simulation. The trade-off is more logistics for anyone providing hardware.

| Aspect | Software hackathon | Robotics / physical AI hackathon |
|---|---|---|
| Skills visible | Coding, APIs, product sense | Same, plus hardware integration and control |
| Typical sponsor contribution | Credits, APIs | Credits, robots, sensors, compute |
| Failure modes you observe | Logic and integration bugs | Also calibration, latency and real-world noise |
| Logistics for sponsors | Low | Higher if you provide hardware |

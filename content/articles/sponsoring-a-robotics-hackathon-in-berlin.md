## Why sponsor a robotics hackathon in Berlin?

Berlin gives a sponsor access to robotics and AI builders inside Germany, the largest robot market in Europe. That matters if you hire for physical AI roles or sell tools to robotics teams in the region.

The market context, from the [International Federation of Robotics World Robotics 2025 release](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years):

| Indicator (2024) | Figure |
|---|---|
| Industrial robot installations in Germany, 2024 | 26,982 units, down 5% |
| Germany's position | Largest robot market in Europe, fifth largest in the world |
| Installations in Europe | 85,000 units, down 8% |
| Installations worldwide | 542,000 units |

A robotics hackathon in Berlin draws on the people behind that market: robotics students, ML engineers, researchers and professionals from the city's companies. For a wider view of the city, see [Berlin's growing robotics ecosystem](/articles/berlins-growing-robotics-ecosystem).

## Who attends a robotics hackathon in Berlin?

At HackLab, attendees are pre-selected builders with robotics, AI or ML backgrounds. Edition #1 attendees included:

## Which Edition #2 track fits which kind of sponsor?

Match your contribution to the track whose teams will use it. A sponsor that fits one track clearly gets more serious projects than one that tries to appeal to all four.

| Track | Sponsors it fits | Possible contribution |
|---|---|---|
| AI Infrastructure | Model, inference, cloud and data-tooling companies | Credits, a challenge on serving or training models for robots |
| Industrial Robotics Simulation | Simulation, manufacturing and automation companies | A simulated work cell, a challenge on a real production task |
| Autonomous Navigation | Mobile robotics, sensor and mapping companies | Sensors or robots, a navigation challenge with a defined course |
| Wildcard / Open Source | Open-source projects and hardware makers | Open hardware, libraries, a challenge to extend an open project |

These are suggestions for planning, not a list of past sponsor challenges. Agree the exact fit with HackLab before you write a brief.

## What is the venue like for sponsors?

HackLab's venue is CIC Berlin at Lohmühlenstraße 65. The [CIC Berlin page](https://cic.com/berlin) describes it as an innovation campus in a 13,000-square-metre former industrial complex next to Görlitzer Park, with 13 event venues on site and 150+ companies based there. It lists Schlesisches Tor (U-Bahn) and Treptower Park (S-Bahn) as nearby stations.

Practical points for sponsor teams:

- **Hardware delivery:** confirm loading access and storage with the organiser before shipping robots.
- **Staffing:** plan shifts, since the build runs continuously.
- **Demo setup:** agree space for arms and a test area for mobile robots in advance.

## How should a company plan a Berlin sponsorship?

Plan it as a short project with one goal and a named owner. The sequence below works for companies based in Berlin and for those travelling in.

1. **Set the goal:** hiring, product adoption or feedback.
2. **Contact the organiser** and agree the contribution: a challenge, hardware or credits, and on-site presence.
3. **Prepare:** challenge brief, tested hardware or credit codes, a staff rota.
4. **Travel and ship** early if your team or hardware comes from outside Berlin.
5. **Run the weekend:** mentor teams, note strong builders, judge if invited.
6. **Follow up** within days with the builders you flagged.

The step-by-step version, including what to prepare before the weekend, is in [how to sponsor a hackathon](/articles/how-to-sponsor-a-hackathon).

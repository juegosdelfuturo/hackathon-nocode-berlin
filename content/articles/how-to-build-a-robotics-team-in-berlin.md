**To build a robotics team in Berlin, hire the roles that unblock your first working prototype, source them from universities, meetups and hackathons as well as job boards, and plan about 15 weeks to bring in a non-EU hire who is still abroad.** Use the probation period of up to six months to confirm fit. Secure space where the team can build and test hardware before the first engineer starts.

This guide covers the sequence, the rules and the local resources. For individual hiring steps, see [how to hire robotics engineers in Berlin](/articles/how-to-hire-robotics-engineers-in-berlin).

## Which roles should a first robotics team include?

A first team needs to cover software, perception, hardware and testing. Most early teams combine roles, so hire for the two or three that block your prototype today.

| Role | Typical scope | Where to look in Berlin |
|---|---|---|
| Robotics software engineer | Control, motion planning, ROS integration | Robotics startups, hackathons |
| Perception / ML engineer | Vision, sensor fusion, learning-based control | AI companies, university labs, hackathons |
| Embedded / electronics engineer | Firmware, motor control, sensors | Hardware makerspaces, applied universities |
| Mechanical engineer | Mechanism design, prototyping, manufacturing | Applied universities, hardware startups |
| Simulation and test engineer | Simulation environments, test pipelines | University labs, hackathon simulation tracks |

For each role, define the two or three skills that matter most for your product before you write the job description.

## In what order should you hire?

Hire in the order your product needs. A common sequence for a hardware product:

1. **Technical lead** who has built a robot end to end and can define the architecture.
2. **Robotics software engineer** to connect control, planning and hardware.
3. **Perception or ML engineer** once the platform moves reliably.
4. **Embedded and mechanical engineers** as you move from off-the-shelf hardware to your own design.
5. **Test and field engineers** before the first customer deployment.

If you start from software on existing robot arms or mobile bases, steps 2 and 3 come first and step 4 can wait.

## Where do you find robotics candidates in Berlin?

Berlin's robotics labour market is software-heavy. The 2025 [Prognos study for Berlin's Senate](https://www.prognos.com/en/project/ai-based-robotics-industrial-city-berlin) counted 149 robotics suppliers with about 6,000 jobs; 56% are software firms and about half have fewer than eleven employees.

This means software, perception and ML candidates are easier to find locally than mechanical and embedded specialists. Plan more sourcing effort for hardware roles.

Sourcing channels to combine:

- **Universities and labs:** TU Berlin, Humboldt-Universität, BHT and Fraunhofer IPK, through thesis projects, student jobs and lab events.
- **Community events:** Berlin Partner's robotics network meetings and local robotics meetups.
- **Hackathons:** HackLab's Edition #1 selected 130 builders from 600+ applications; Edition #2 selected 200 from 1,100+ registrations.
- **International hiring:** the EU Blue Card for candidates from outside the EU.

## How long does hiring from outside the EU take?

Plan about 15 weeks from start to entry. Berlin's immigration office runs the [accelerated skilled worker procedure](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/service/business-immigration-service/beschleunigtes-fachkraefte-verfahren/), which the employer starts on the candidate's behalf. Its target timeline is about 8 weeks for recognition of qualifications, 1 week for Federal Employment Agency approval, and 3 weeks each for the visa appointment and issuance.

Salary must meet the [EU Blue Card](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/324659/en/) threshold. In 2026 that is €45,934.20 gross per year for shortage occupations, including engineers and ICT specialists, and €50,700 for other roles. Only fixed pay counts.

The full process is in [hiring international engineers in Germany](/articles/hiring-international-engineers-in-germany).

## How does the probation period work in Germany?

An agreed probation period can last up to six months. Under [Section 622 of the German Civil Code (BGB)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__622.html), either side can end the employment during that period with two weeks' notice. After probation, the basic statutory notice period is four weeks to the 15th or the end of a calendar month.

Use the probation period with a written plan:

1. Agree on a concrete first deliverable, such as a subsystem running on real hardware.
2. Review progress at fixed points, for example after month two and month four.
3. Decide before the six months end.

## Where can a robotics team work and prototype in Berlin?

You need space for both desks and hardware. Two options with different strengths:

- **[MotionLab.Berlin](https://motionlab.berlin/)** is a makerspace and innovation hub for hardtech with prototyping labs, machinery, workspaces and an event space across several Berlin sites, and a community of 2,000+.
- **[CIC Berlin](https://cic.com/berlin)** at Lohmühlenstraße 65 lists private offices from €1,800 per month for teams of 1 to 100+, with 24/7 access, plus coworking from €250 per month. It does not list lab space for Berlin.

Check floor loading, power and delivery access before you move robots into any office space.

## What does a first-year hiring plan look like?

A simple plan that combines the steps above:

| Phase | Focus | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1–2 | Foundations | Define the first prototype, hire the technical lead, secure workspace |
| Months 2–4 | Core team | Hire robotics software and perception roles; start fast-track cases for non-EU candidates |
| Months 4–8 | Hardware | Add embedded and mechanical engineers; build community presence through meetups |
| Months 8–12 | Testing | Add test and field roles; sponsor or join a hackathon to fill the next roles |

Adjust the order to your product, but start non-EU cases early because of the 15-week timeline.

## How do you test robotics candidates before hiring?

Test candidates on practical work, not only interviews. Options include a paid take-home task on simulation or real data, a pairing session on your codebase, or watching candidates build at a hackathon.

**HackLab is attended by pre-selected robotics, AI and ML builders. At Edition #1, they were robotics students finishing their degree, people with machine-learning backgrounds, robotics professionals across specialties, master's students building their first real prototypes, and university researchers.** Every one of them passed selection: 130 of 600+ applicants at Edition #1 and 200 of 1,100+ registrants at Edition #2. Companies meet them while they build, not through a CV pile.

## What profiles does HackLab attract?

HackLab Robotics groups its Edition #1 participants into five profiles. The table pairs each profile with the kind of hiring need a company might bring to it. That pairing is general guidance, not HackLab data about specific people.

| Participant profile (HackLab Ed. #1) | Typical hiring fit for a company |
|---|---|
| Robotics students finishing their degree | Graduate and junior robotics roles |
| People with machine-learning backgrounds | ML roles moving towards robotics or physical AI |
| Robotics professionals across specialties | Experienced engineering hires |
| Master's students building first real prototypes | Internships, thesis projects, junior roles |
| University researchers | Research-oriented roles and R&D collaboration |

How many participants fall into each profile, and their gender or nationality mix, are not covered here.

## How are HackLab participants selected?

Participants are selected from a much larger pool of applicants. At Edition #1, HackLab selected 130 builders from 600+ applications. For Edition #2, it selected 200 builders from 1,100+ registrations on Luma.

The exact selection criteria are not covered here. What a sponsor can rely on is that the people in the room have already been filtered from a larger pool.

## What will companies see participants do?

Companies see participants build working prototypes in 36–48 hours. At Edition #1, about 20 teams demoed close to 20 final solutions to judges and sponsor companies.

At Edition #2, builders work across four tracks: AI Infrastructure, Industrial Robotics Simulation, Autonomous Navigation, and Wildcard / Open Source. The hardware on site includes robot arms, robot sets and a Unitree Go2 EDU quadruped. The track a builder picks is itself a signal of where their interests lie. [HackLab Edition #2 tracks explained](/articles/hacklab-edition-2-tracks-explained) maps each track to skill areas.

## Why does the mix of profiles matter for hiring?

A mixed field lets one sponsor meet candidates for several roles at one event. Because students, ML practitioners, professionals and researchers all attended Edition #1, a company does not need separate channels for graduates and experienced hires.

Practical ways to use the mix:

- **Graduate hiring.** Robotics students finishing their degree, and master's students, can be seen working on real prototypes before they enter the job market. More on this in [hiring junior robotics engineers and graduates](/articles/hiring-junior-robotics-engineers-and-graduates).
- **Cross-skilling.** People with ML backgrounds who are building on robotics hardware show how they handle the move from software to physical systems.
- **Senior hiring.** Robotics professionals across specialties show how they work in a team under a deadline.
- **Research links.** University researchers can become a route into collaboration, not only hiring.

## How should companies evaluate HackLab participants?

Watch the work, not only the final demo. A 36–48 hour build shows how a person scopes a problem, splits work with teammates and recovers when the hardware does not behave.

A simple approach for a sponsor team:

1. Decide in advance which one or two roles you are hiring for.
2. Spend time with teams in the tracks closest to those roles.
3. Note individual contributions, not just the team result.
4. Meet promising builders in person before the final demos.
5. Follow up quickly after the event.

A fuller method is in [how to evaluate candidates at a hackathon](/articles/how-to-evaluate-candidates-at-a-hackathon).

## Have HackLab participants been hired?

Yes. After Edition #1, 5 participants were hired by sponsor companies. Which companies hired them, and for what roles, is not covered here.

## How HackLab helps companies meet robotics and AI builders

HackLab gives companies direct access to pre-selected builders: 130 from 600+ at Edition #1, and 200 from 1,100+ at Edition #2. Sponsors can set challenges, provide hardware or credits, and meet builders in person while they build. To reach these profiles at a future edition, [partner with HackLab](/for-companies).

**Berlin's robotics research is concentrated at TU Berlin, Humboldt-Universität, the Berliner Hochschule für Technik (BHT) and Fraunhofer IPK, with Freie Universität, HTW Berlin and Charité also active.** TU Berlin hosts the Robotics and Biology Laboratory and, with Humboldt-Universität, the Science of Intelligence cluster. BHT runs a dedicated humanoid robotics degree from its Haus der Robotik. For employers, these institutions are the main source of robotics graduates, PhD-level researchers and research partners in the city.

## Which institutions do robotics research in Berlin?

Berlin Partner's [Business Location Center](https://businesslocationcenter.de/en/industry/robotik) lists these research and academic institutions in its robotics overview, among others:

- Technische Universität Berlin
- Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin
- Freie Universität Berlin
- Berliner Hochschule für Technik (BHT)
- Hochschule für Technik und Wirtschaft (HTW Berlin)
- Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin (involved in the EU project TEF-Health)
- Fraunhofer Institute for Production Systems and Design Technology (IPK)

The table summarises the institutions covered in detail below.

| Institution | Robotics focus | Relevance for hiring |
|---|---|---|
| TU Berlin – Robotics and Biology Lab | Manipulation, soft robotic hands | PhD researchers in manipulation |
| Science of Intelligence (TU Berlin + HU Berlin) | Natural and artificial intelligence, multi-robot research | 145 researchers, 220 robots |
| BHT – Haus der Robotik | Humanoid robotics, soft robotics, locomotion, exoskeletons | Bachelor graduates trained on humanoid hardware |
| Fraunhofer IPK | Automation and digitally integrated production | Applied researchers in production automation |
| Robotics Institute Germany | National network; TU Berlin is a core member | Links Berlin labs to 13 other core institutions |

## What does TU Berlin's Robotics and Biology Laboratory work on?

The lab's public work centres on robotic manipulation, especially soft hands. The [Robotics and Biology Laboratory (RBO)](https://www.tu.berlin/en/robotics) reports that its RBO Hand 2 paper received the RSS Test of Time Award in July 2026, and that it had two accepted papers and three workshop talks at ICRA 2026.

The lab also lists open positions, including a PhD position in robotics and AI. Its graduates and postdocs are a direct source of manipulation expertise.

## What is the Science of Intelligence cluster?

[Science of Intelligence (SCIoI)](https://www.scienceofintelligence.de/) is a Cluster of Excellence studying intelligence, "whether natural or artificial, whether individual, social or collective." It is funded by the German Research Foundation (DFG) under Germany's Excellence Strategy.

Its scale, as stated by the cluster:

- 470 publications

- 52 research projects in 4 research units
- 145 researchers
- 6 laboratories and 6 cluster professorships
- 220 robots, including Kilobots, Naos, Peppers, Pandas and Thymios

SCIoI is a [joint project](https://www.scienceofintelligence.de/about/participating-institutions/) of TU Berlin and Humboldt-Universität, with Universität Potsdam, Freie Universität Berlin, Charité and the Max Planck Institute for Human Development as partners.

## What does BHT's Haus der Robotik offer companies?

BHT's [Haus der Robotik](https://www.berlin.de/industriestadt/masterplan-industriestadt-berlin/blog/artikel.1583251.php) at Kurfürstenstraße 141 opened officially on 15 July 2025. It houses the labs of the humanoid robotics degree programme on three levels: a workshop and locomotion lab, a manipulation level focused on hand development, and a neurorobotics lab.

Facts relevant to employers, from the berlin.de report:

1. **Bachelor's in humanoid robotics** since 2018, with 44 places per year, about three times oversubscribed.
2. **A master's programme** is being set up, and BHT has received doctoral rights for AI-based robotics.
3. **Research areas** include human-robot interaction, soft robotics, bipedal and quadrupedal walking, cognitive robotics and exoskeletons for assembly-line workers.
4. **Company contact:** the house works with companies, especially on exoskeleton projects, and plans a monthly "Roboterstammtisch" for companies and researchers and a coworking space for spin-offs.

## What does Fraunhofer IPK contribute?

[Fraunhofer IPK](https://www.ipk.fraunhofer.de/en.html), at Pascalstraße in Berlin, is a research partner for "digitally integrated production." Its research topics include production processes and facilities and automation. It names TU Berlin as a local partner and publishes the customer magazine of the Production Technology Center Berlin.

For companies building industrial robots or automation cells, IPK covers applied production research.

## How are Berlin's labs connected to Germany's national robotics network?

Through the Robotics Institute Germany. The [University of Bonn's announcement](https://uni-bonn.de/en/news/robotics-institute-germany-pools-top-level-research) describes RIG as a consortium of leading German robotics centres focused on AI-based robotics, launched on 1 July 2024 with €20 million from the Federal Ministry of Education and Research for four years.

TU Munich leads it. TU Berlin is one of the 14 core institutions, alongside KIT, RWTH Aachen, DLR, the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems and DFKI, among others.

Berlin also hosts [BIFOLD](https://bifold.berlin/), a machine learning and big data research institute with TU Berlin among its institutional partners. It is relevant for ML hiring rather than robotics hardware.

## How can companies collaborate with these institutions?

Three collaboration models appear in Berlin's research landscape:

1. **Applied research projects** with institutes such as Fraunhofer IPK, which presents itself as a research partner for production companies.
2. **Thesis and student projects** at universities, where a student works on a company problem.
3. **Joint projects with industry partners,** such as the Palm-E project at TU Berlin with Google listed by the Business Location Center.

Each model gives the company access to researchers before they enter the job market.

## How can companies reach graduates from these institutions?

Companies reach them through research collaborations, open lab events and hands-on events. Practical options:

- Join planned formats such as BHT's Roboterstammtisch and monthly lab visits.
- Offer thesis topics or student jobs connected to a real product problem.
- Meet students and researchers where they build. HackLab's Edition #1 participants included robotics students finishing their degree, master's students building first real prototypes and university researchers.

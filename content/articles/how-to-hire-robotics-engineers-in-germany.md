**To hire robotics engineers in Germany, treat it as a competitive national market: source directly in the regional robotics clusters, widen the search to non-EU engineers through the EU Blue Card, and use hands-on events to see candidates build.** Germany is Europe's largest robot market, and demand for engineers who can program those robots is high. Companies that wait for applications on job boards lose candidates to employers who reach out first.

## How big is Germany's robotics market?

Germany is the largest robot market in Europe and the fifth-largest in the world. According to the [International Federation of Robotics](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years), Germany installed 26,982 industrial robots in 2024, down 5% on the record year 2023 but still about 32% of all installations in Europe. [Germany Trade & Invest](https://www.gtai.de/en/meta/press/germany-is-europe-s-leading-robotics-nation--1933322) reports a robot density of 449 industrial robots per 10,000 manufacturing employees, the fourth highest in the world.

| Unfilled IT positions in Germany, 2026 | 79,000 | Bitkom |

Every installed robot needs people to integrate, program, maintain and improve it. That is why robotics engineers in Germany are hired from the same small pool by OEMs, suppliers, integrators and startups at once. For more numbers, see [Germany's robotics industry in facts](/articles/germany-robotics-industry-facts).

## How tight is the market for engineers in Germany?

The broader tech labour market has eased but is still short of people. In a [Bitkom survey published in September 2026](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/IT-Fachkraeftemangel-in-den-letzten-drei-Jahren-halbiert), German companies reported 79,000 unfilled IT positions, down from 109,000 in 2025 and 149,000 in 2023. Even so, 78% of companies still observe a shortage of IT specialists, and 31% of companies with open IT roles receive practically no applications.

Robotics engineers are a subset of this market with extra requirements: hardware experience, control theory, real-time systems or robot learning. The easing of the general IT market does not mean robotics roles are easy to fill.

## Where should you look for robotics engineers in Germany?

Look in regional clusters, because German robotics talent is spread across the country rather than concentrated in one city. The main pools are:

- **Research networks.** The Robotics Institute Germany (RIG), led by the Technical University of Munich, brings together 16 full partners and 19 associated partners. It received [€20 million from the Federal Ministry of Education and Research](https://www.tum.de/en/news-and-events/all-news/press-releases/details/robotics-institute-germany-established) for July 2024 to June 2028, and one of its stated goals is promoting talent and training.
- **Manufacturing regions.** Automotive, machinery and electronics producers employ automation and integration engineers who know production lines.
- **Startup hubs.** Cities such as Berlin host robotics and AI startups where engineers work across perception, planning and learning.
- **Students and graduates.** Master's and PhD students at technical universities often build their first full robot systems in thesis projects.
- **Hackathons and build events.** These show you how a candidate works with hardware under time pressure. See [hackathons in Germany for companies hiring tech talent](/articles/hackathons-in-germany-for-companies-hiring-tech-talent).

## How do you hire robotics engineers from outside the EU?

Use the EU Blue Card, a residence permit for qualified workers from non-EU countries. According to the [City of Stuttgart Welcome Center](https://www.stuttgart.de/en/microsite/welcome-center/ankommen/aufenthalt/blaue-karte-eu), two salary limits apply in 2026:

| EU Blue Card route (2026) | Minimum gross salary per year |
|---|---|
| General threshold | €50,700 |
| Shortage occupations (e.g. STEM) and graduates of the last three years | €45,934.20 |

These are legal minimums, not market rates. Treat them as a floor and set the actual salary from your own benchmarks for the role.

The reduced threshold matters most for junior roles, because it makes recent international graduates eligible at a lower salary. Confirm the figures with the responsible immigration office before you make an offer, because they are adjusted every year.

A practical sequence for a non-EU hire:

1. Check the candidate's eligibility for the Blue Card with the responsible immigration office early, before the final interview.
2. Make a written offer at or above the applicable threshold.
3. Plan the start date around visa processing, and keep the candidate engaged with the team while they wait.
4. Offer relocation help: housing search, registration and paperwork.

## What does a competitive offer for a German robotics engineer include?

A competitive offer combines a clear technical mission with practical support. Engineers compare offers on:

- **The problem and the hardware.** Which robot, which task, and how often they touch real hardware.
- **Team language.** English-speaking teams can recruit internationally; German-only teams narrow the pool.
- **Location and flexibility.** Hardware work requires on-site time, so state the expected days in the lab.
- **Relocation support.** Help with visas, housing and registration shortens the time to start.
- **Growth.** Conference budget, publishing, or open-source work matters to research-minded candidates.

## Should you use a recruiter for robotics roles in Germany?

Use a recruiter for senior and leadership roles with a precise specification, and source directly for everything else. Specialised robotics recruiters can reach passive senior candidates across regions.

For most engineering roles, though, a direct message from an engineer on your team, with a specific technical problem, gets a better response than a generic agency approach. Whichever route you use, keep the practical assessment in-house so your own engineers judge the work.

## How long should the hiring process take?

Keep it as short as you can while still including one practical step. A typical structure is an intro call, a technical conversation, a practical task (simulation, code review or a build session) and a final meeting with the team. Each extra round gives a competing employer time to make an offer first.
